Figures and claims: the proposal

  • Entergy’s commitment and the project figures: APSC Order No. 4, Docket 25-054-U (March 3, 2026), summarizing Entergy’s testimony: Entergy “will work with its Engineering, Procurement, and Construction (EPC) Contractor to use local contractors and suppliers when possible”; as many as 800 people on site at peak construction; about $46 million in sales and use tax during construction; 10–15 Entergy staff at the facility; about $9.6 million in property taxes in the first year of operation. Also Entergy’s construction notice (construction began on or about July 10, 2026).
  • APSC, Generating Arkansas Jobs Act annual report (June 30, 2026): approved cost $1,601,500,000; in service December 2028; 700–800 construction jobs; 15 permanent jobs.
  • “Arkansans building Arkansas” and local firms “whenever feasible”: Entergy Arkansas’s vendor fair announcement (fair held March 25, 2026, Pine Bluff Convention Center, for Jefferson Power Station and Arkansas Cypress); Pine Bluff Commercial coverage; Entergy’s April 9, 2026 recap (almost 340 company representatives at the March fairs).
  • How Entergy buys: supplier registration steps, supplier engagement, the Second-Tier Initiative (prime suppliers report local subcontracting quarterly) and Avetta safety prequalification for on-site work. Requirements shown in the walkthrough are typical examples, not Entergy’s or Wanzek’s actual list.
  • County context: U.S. Census Bureau County Business Patterns 2023 for Jefferson County, Arkansas (NAICS 722 food services and drinking places, 100 establishments; NAICS 23 construction, 64, of which 4 are heavy and civil engineering; NAICS 484 truck transportation, 16, of which 12 have fewer than five employees); American Community Survey 2020–2024 5-year estimates, table B17001 (poverty 18.6% county, 12.5% U.S.); population 90,718 in the 1980 census and 62,987 in the Vintage 2025 estimate.
  • Workforce: Entergy Arkansas’s commitment to historically Black colleges and universities, including the University of Arkansas at Pine Bluff.
  • The crew meal program: workers stopped leaving the grounds once a caterer fed them on site at Meta’s Louisiana build, reported by Fortune (March 26, 2026); transportation incidents caused more than a third of construction-worker deaths in 2023 (CPWR, via Roads & Bridges); OSHA heat guidance on water, rest and shade; Arkansas Department of Health food protection rules for mobile and catered food; the GSA FY2026 rate for Jefferson County, $68 a day for meals and incidentals. The model’s inputs are our assumptions: share who eat (45%), price ($14), months at peak (7.5), and 21.7 working days a month.
  • The workstream models and “What Jefferson County Keeps”: a forecast, run 4,000 times. Every input is a low, most likely and high value. Facts (crew size, pay, site-work share, acres) are the same with or without the program; levers (local hire, county firms’ share of site work and upkeep, county kitchens’ share of meals, sites with backup power) have one range without a program and one with Built to Stay. Capacity after the build counts residents still in the trade (about 35% without a program, 55% with one, earning about $15,000 a year more for about 10 years), county firms with a contract record winning new work (about 3 without, 15 with, about $150,000 a year each) and county kitchens adding catering (about 1 without, 4 with, about $75,000 a year each); these are our estimates, to be checked by following them for three years after the build. The page reports the most likely difference and the range 8 in 10 runs land inside; the ranges are our judgment, anchored to the builds below. Local hire: 11% at a Martin County, Kentucky solar build with no program (Mountain Citizen, 2024), about 35% assumed with a program, against 41% in the Pennsylvania study and on the Gordie Howe bridge (above) and 50% as a Kern County, California permit standard; 85% at Honeysuckle Solar, Indiana, through union halls (Lightsource bp), a stretch case we do not use. Permanent pay: O*NET Arkansas wages for electrical and electronics repairers, powerhouse, substation and relay (10th percentile to median). Site work and upkeep costs: NREL cost benchmarks and operations studies (77811, 83586, 85418, 89127); Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory’s Utility-Scale Solar, 2026 edition. Outage costs: LBNL’s Interruption Cost Estimate 2.0 report (October 2026). Hotel nights: the GSA FY2026 lodging rate, $110. All figures in today’s dollars, not discounted.
  • “What Grows Around It” (0.0.126): single point values, each editable. Property tax: $9.6 million in year 1 (APSC Order No. 4) falling evenly to 20% by year 30; Jefferson County millage from the DFA 2025 millage report; assessment at 20% by the APSC Tax Division; the 25-mill uniform school rate offsets state aid (DESE funding guide); Act 9 payments in lieu of tax (Kutak Rock). Craft pay: FRED, Arkansas construction hourly earnings. Local hire without a program: Badger State Solar, about 14% (USDA Appendix N); apprentice labor-hour rule (SEIA). Pay gain: BLS OEWS, Pine Bluff. Local re-spending: IMPLAN factors from a rural Kentucky solar county (Livingston County analysis). Crew lunch demand: ezCater survey. The staffing curve is our design; Wanzek’s schedule would replace it. Jefferson Power Station: Arkansas Times (APSC approval, January 28, 2026), Entergy Arkansas (754 MW, 700–800 peak workers, about 22 permanent jobs, online 2029, about $128 million in local, county and sales taxes). What grows around a plant: Enel’s Texas solar grazing contract; Entergy Power Through; Entergy Mississippi and C Spire fiber; Batesville schools solar; Borrego Springs microgrid; Entergy charger grant, Pine Bluff.
  • Targets: our own, built from Entergy’s job figures (100 residents is about one in eight of 800 peak jobs) and the county business counts above. Not commitments.
  • Where someone owned it: Gordie Howe International Bridge community benefits report, 2024–2025 (41% local workforce against a 20% goal); Louisiana’s Accelerator supplier-readiness program and Source Louisiana; Meta’s Richland Parish figures (more than $875 million with more than 160 Louisiana businesses since the December 2024 groundbreaking, 84% local to Northeast Louisiana; Meta’s own numbers).

Figures and claims: the proposal

  • Arkansas Public Service Commission, Docket 25-054-U: Order No. 4 (Mar 2026), approving 600 MW of solar and a 350 MW battery and naming Wanzek Construction as EPC contractor; Entergy’s construction notice; Entergy’s application (about 3,000 acres; 73 directly affected landowners).
  • APSC, Generating Arkansas Jobs Act annual report (June 30, 2026): approved cost $1,601,500,000; in service December 2028; revenue requirement $33.7 million (2026) rising to $146.0 million (2029); 700–800 construction jobs at $5,000–$8,000 a month; 15 permanent jobs.
  • APSC Docket 25-055-P, Order No. 9: 30-year expected life; about $9.6 million first-year property tax; about $46 million construction sales and use tax. APSC quarterly report: Entergy Arkansas’s authorized return on equity, 9.65%.
  • The value split. From Entergy: its unredacted revenue requirement for Arkansas Cypress (Docket 25-054-U, Doc. 111, Oct 15, 2026), The page uses 2026–2058, the construction years plus the 30-year expected life in Order No. 9: about $3.11 billion, of which about $1.70 billion is return on rate base and construction work in progress (shareholders, lenders and income tax, at a 6.75% before-tax return), $1.54 billion is the plant’s cost returned through depreciation, $393 million is operations and maintenance, and about −$516 million is other credits. The schedule itself runs on to 2088, because a small share of longer-lived plant (about $2 million a year of depreciation) and the land stay in rate base after 2058. Those years add about $130 million, bringing the full schedule to about $3.24 billion. Our estimates, labelled as such on the page: equipment and contractor share of the build, based on U.S. Department of Energy cost benchmarks; landowners at University of Arkansas Extension and National Agricultural Law Center typical lease ranges ($450–$1,600 an acre a year) on 3,000 acres with a 2% escalator, overstated where land was bought rather than leased; local firms at 8–15% of the build; local wages from the job counts and pay above. No family’s lease terms are used.
  • County context: U.S. Census Bureau population (1980–2020 censuses; Vintage 2025 estimates); American Community Survey 2020–2024 (median household income, poverty, via Census Reporter); County Business Patterns 2023 (100 food services, 64 construction, 16 trucking, 1,199 establishments).
  • Food model: every input is our assumption to confirm with the contractor (120–150 worker-bought lunches on per diem, or about 800 workers × 2 meals; $14–15 a meal; 260 working days). The GSA FY2026 rate for Jefferson County is $68 a day for meals and incidentals.
  • Supplier programs: Entergy Second-Tier Initiative, supplier engagement, Avetta registration. Precedent: Meta (above); Big River Steel (above); Google Energy Impact Fund ($25 million, Crittenden County); Steel River ($5 million from Google, $3 million from Cypress Creek Energy).

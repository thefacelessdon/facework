How It Would Be Run

The governance, measures, precedent and fallbacks behind the proposal. Back to the proposal →

01

Who Decides,
and What It Never Does

  • Decisions.

    A small working group picks pilots and can stop any of them: one person each from Entergy (community affairs or landowner relations), the contractor, a training partner and a local business, plus the coordinator. Nothing starts without the responsible organization’s written agreement.

  • Staffing.

    In Phase 1 the coordinator is Stedmon Harper of Delta Futurism, and Delta Futurism is not seeking a paid role. Pilot staffing and budgets are set with partners in Phase 2, before anything launches.

  • Procurement and liability.

    The contractor alone decides who it hires and buys from. The partnership helps businesses get ready to bid. It does not vouch for, guarantee or insure any business, and it is not a party to any contract between a business and Entergy or the contractor.

  • Data and privacy.

    Trainee and business records are kept only with each person’s or firm’s written consent, used only for this program, never sold, and deleted on request. Partner reports show totals, never individuals.

  • Reporting.

    A one-page quarterly report to partners: what was tried, what it produced, and what stopped. Partners review it before anything is published.

  • Fit with existing programs.

    This feeds Entergy’s supplier registration, Supplier Engagement, the Second-Tier Initiative, Avetta and community affairs, and the contractor’s own local-hiring and subcontracting plans. It replaces none of them. If Entergy already runs something similar, we join it.

  • Conflicts.

    Delta Futurism comes from a landowner family whose businesses may bid on project work. When one does, family members step out of choosing or assessing that opportunity, and the business goes through the same readiness check and contractor process as any other.

  • Lease and payments.

    Nothing here asks to change any lease, payment or project term, and nothing in the partnership depends on any family’s lease.

03

There Is
Precedent

Energy developers and their large customers already fund community capability next to their projects. These would be starting points for the funding conversation in Phase 3.

  • Energy Impact Fund · Google, West Memphis.

    $25 million announced in October 2025 with Google’s West Memphis data center, for energy efficiency and affordability for residents of Crittenden County and the surrounding area, paid out through grantee organizations. Question: nothing public includes Jefferson County. Could the fund reach it, or does Arkansas Cypress need its own mechanism?

  • Steel River Energy Center · Mississippi County.

    $8 million in community commitments alongside a solar project: $5 million from Google and $3 million from Cypress Creek Energy, including support for the local school district. Question: does Arkansas Cypress have a comparable commitment that isn’t public yet?

  • Supplier development · Entergy.

    Entergy’s Second-Tier Initiative, which aims to “maximize opportunities for local suppliers through prime suppliers,” plus supplier registration and a supplier engagement and development team. Question: can local firms in workstreams B and C count toward it on Arkansas Cypress?

04

If Things
Go Differently

  • The build moves faster than the pilots.

    Start with food and suppliers, which have the shortest lead time. Workforce follows the contractor’s next hiring wave and the Jefferson Power Station.

  • A caterer or supplier is already under contract.

    Local firms bid as subcontractors to the incumbent, or prepare for the next phase and the next project.

  • No paid placements are committed.

    No one is trained for a job that isn’t there. Training starts only with an employer’s commitment in writing.

  • The schedule changes.

    Phases are keyed to construction milestones, not calendar dates.

Where the portfolio is heading. And what that means as a SoundCloud partner.

Read from the Marshall aLIVE and CACHE proposals, the HUE proposal system, An Operating Doctrine for Cultural Architecture (Feb 2025), and this week’s Delta Futurism work. The ventures aren’t one umbrella; the proposal speaks for HUE only.

  • HUEFrom one-off campaigns to programs that keep running: aLIVE’s one room a month with a month of content after and a writers’ hub; Frequency, HUE SOUND Camp, BOUNCE; HUE FM’s ordered, human-picked listening.
  • CACHECreative gigs into paid work that comes back: “Real jobs for the region’s creatives. Partners who come back for more.”
  • Delta FuturismThe same idea on a power build: local workers, kitchens and suppliers ready to win the work and still working after it.
  • Cultural architectureProduction makes motion; architecture decides whether it compounds. Long-view work is “nobody’s job,” and “if it is housed inside a single institution, it inherits that institution’s incentives and constraints.”
  • For SoundCloudSoundCloud has the activity and the money tools. No one owns what happens between a breakout month, a Buzzing spike and a scene report. HUE builds that layer.

04

A service partner, or an independent entity? Lead with A. Build it so it can become B.

A: HUE is hired or co-funded to run creative ops, editorial and program design with SoundCloud. B: an independent scene-and-artist institution (editorial, stations, cohort, archive), anchored by SoundCloud but owned and governed outside it, open to other partners.

Each test, side by side
TestA · Service partnerB · Independent entity
Fits Kalesha’s stageYes. A workshop call, then a small first run she can say yes to.No. Needs executives and legal, two or three steps up.
Fits the call for contributorsYes. Blogs, creators, pages, writers and streamers need briefing, scheduling and publishing; that ops layer is a service SoundCloud can start now.14Indirect. An entity can host contributors, but adds governance before anyone is briefed.
What SoundCloud is buyingCreator content, ambassadors, brand formats.1216Editorial memory, but it bought that in-house with Nina.11
Cultural-architecture fitWeak alone: inside one institution, the work bends to its campaign cycles.Strong. Holds continuity across cycles; sees across efforts without delivering any one.
CredibilityMedium; can read as SoundCloud marketing.High; independent editorial reads as taste, not ads.
Owner-change riskA new owner can cut vendors.45Outlives an owner change.
MoneyA clear budget line; brand co-funding.Several funders; slower, not tied to one buyer.
Portfolio directionA service lane: revenue and proof.Matches where HUE is heading: programs that keep running.
Recommendation: lead with the service partnership. Don’t pitch B to Kalesha now.
  1. Stage. Kalesha can open a pilot; she can’t charter an institution. Pitching B first aims two steps past her.
  2. The call is an ops problem. A roster of blogs, creators, pages, writers and streamers only works with someone briefing them, getting the work out and publishing it well. That is HUE’s creative operations, and it’s a service, so A is the honest shape of the first yes.
  3. Keep the long-view assets outside SoundCloud from day one. Frequency, HUE FM, the scene record and artist relationships stay HUE’s, with SoundCloud as anchor partner and release home. That is the seed of B, without asking for it.
  4. Owner risk argues for independence. A vendor contract is fragile through a sale; an outlet with its own audience keeps going.
  5. Raise B after a pilot season, with numbers, as Grow it and Own it: an independent scene institution with SoundCloud as founding partner, open to brand co-funding.
  6. Don’t give away in A: no exclusivity on Frequency or HUE FM, no transfer of the archive or artist contacts, no promise any named artist joins.

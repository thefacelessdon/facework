# FVR-103 — Knowledge Artifacts

## Question

What visual and structural decisions make knowledge durable, revisitable, and
capable of outliving its original tool?

## Reference field

Laboratory notebooks, marginalia, academic papers, engineering drawings, UNIX
manual pages, Markdown files, commonplace books, archival finding aids, version
control, and linked-note systems.

## Observations

1. Durable artifacts distinguish content from the interface used to create it.
2. Dates, authorship, version, source, and status make later interpretation
   possible.
3. Stable structure permits both human reading and machine transformation.
4. Revision history reveals how confidence and meaning changed.
5. Notes can remain provisional without becoming disposable.
6. Links are most valuable when the relationship is named: source, contradiction,
   extension, implementation, or evidence.

## Extracted principles

### Ideas earn permanence

Observation, note, model, framework, standard, and constitutional principle are
different states of knowledge. The visual system should make those states
perceptible.

### Files before interfaces

Canonical knowledge should have a durable source representation independent of
the website, editor, or model currently mediating it.

### Provenance is part of meaning

A claim without date, status, and lineage may look finished while remaining
impossible to evaluate.

### Revision should remain visible

Superseded does not mean erased. Later versions need the context of earlier
decisions.

## Limits and cautions

- Plain text is durable but cannot preserve every spatial or sensory property.
- Link abundance can create a network that is traversable but not understandable.
- Version metadata becomes noise when status vocabulary is inconsistent.
- “Notebook” aesthetics can manufacture intimacy or incompleteness without real
  process evidence.

## FVS impact

- Supports the lineage schema and status model.
- Supports the strategist's-notebook direction for face.works, provided the site
  exposes genuine observation and revision rather than staged roughness.
- Supports paper-mode typography and print-capable documents.
- Requires durable text descriptions for diagrams and motion-dependent claims.

# FVR-000 — Atlas of Coherence

The Atlas is a research system for discovering visual relationships that produce
coherence across natural, cultural, and engineered contexts.

It is not a mood board. It does not ask whether Facework should look like a
reference. It asks:

> What principle of coherence does this demonstrate, under what conditions, and
> with what limits?

## Research domains

- biological morphology and living networks;
- scientific and mathematical visualization;
- information architecture and wayfinding;
- maps, notation, manuals, and standards;
- architecture and industrial systems;
- typography and editorial systems;
- interfaces and operating systems;
- cultural artifacts, archives, and performance;
- governance symbols and public infrastructure.

## Study method

### 1. Observe

Record the artifact, maker, date, context, audience, medium, and function without
interpreting its appearance as a Facework direction.

### 2. Describe

Identify the actual relationships: axis, distribution, boundary, hierarchy,
sequence, rhythm, density, adaptation, and change.

### 3. Explain

State what problem the relationship solves and the conditions under which it
works.

### 4. Challenge

Identify exclusions, failures, cultural assumptions, misleading readings, and
conditions where the pattern breaks.

### 5. Extract

Formulate a transferable principle without copying the artifact's surface form.

### 6. Test

Apply the principle to at least two distinct Facework artifact classes.

### 7. Decide

Record whether the finding supports, weakens, or leaves unchanged an FVS rule.

## Evidence standard

A study distinguishes:

- **observation** — directly perceivable in the artifact;
- **source claim** — documented by a reliable source;
- **interpretation** — Facework's reading of the relationship;
- **hypothesis** — a proposition requiring further testing;
- **decision impact** — an explicit consequence for the specification.

No isolated artifact can establish a universal principle. Cross-domain recurrence
strengthens a hypothesis; testing in Facework determines whether it is useful.

## Admission criteria

A reference enters the Atlas only when it has:

1. identifiable provenance;
2. a clear relation to a research question;
3. a written structural observation;
4. a limitation or counter-reading;
5. a potential or actual FVS decision impact.

“It feels right” is not sufficient.

## Output

Each completed study uses the [reference-study template](../templates/reference-study.md)
and may propose:

- a new or revised definition;
- a primitive interaction;
- a geometric or compositional rule;
- an application requirement;
- a test for an existing identity artifact;
- no change, when evidence is insufficient.

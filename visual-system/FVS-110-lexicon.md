# FVS-110 — Visual Lexicon

This lexicon fixes the meaning of recurring terms. Implementations may use
ordinary language publicly, but specification work uses these definitions.

## Core terms

### Artifact

A bounded output through which Facework communicates or operates: a page,
diagram, document, mark, interface, motion sequence, object, or environment.

### Behavior

The way a visual relationship responds to content, context, scale, state, or
time. Behavior is more durable than appearance.

### Canonical

Approved as the reference definition or implementation at a stated version.
Canonical does not mean immutable.

### Coherence

The condition in which parts reinforce a governing whole and their relationships
remain intelligible. Coherence is not sameness, polish, neatness, or symmetry.

### Component

A reusable assembly with a defined purpose, anatomy, states, constraints, and
relationship to the larger system.

### Composition

The arrangement of relationships within a bounded artifact, including hierarchy,
sequence, density, rhythm, alignment, and space.

### Constraint

A declared limit that protects meaning, usability, continuity, or integrity.
A constraint is not a habit disguised as a rule.

### Continuity

The preservation of identity and intelligible relationship through change.

### Derivation

The documented chain from meaning through principle, primitive, geometry,
component, composition, behavior, and artifact.

### Evidence

Observed results or comparative findings that support, weaken, or refine a
visual proposition. Repetition is not evidence by itself.

### Expression

A context-specific manifestation of the system. Multiple expressions may be
equally conforming.

### Geometry

The measurable spatial relationships that organize form: axis, interval,
proportion, alignment, grid, boundary, scale, and transformation.

### Invariant

A relationship that must survive changes in expression for identity and meaning
to remain intact.

### Legibility

The degree to which an intended audience can perceive, distinguish, navigate,
and understand an artifact.

### Lineage

The recorded relationship between an artifact, its sources, governing rules,
versions, decisions, and successors.

### Mode

A coordinated environmental expression, such as light, dark, print, projection,
or low-bandwidth. A mode changes tokens without changing semantics.

### Pattern

A recurring solution to a recurring relational problem, supported by evidence
across more than one instance.

### Primitive

An irreducible visual behavior used to construct more complex relationships.
Primitives are not icons, motifs, or protocol phases.

### Reference implementation

An approved demonstration of one valid way to implement the specification. It
is evidence and guidance, not the only acceptable appearance.

### Rhythm

The recurrence and variation of intervals through space, content, or time.

### Semantic color

Color whose role communicates a defined state or category and remains consistent
across contexts. Semantic color must not be the only carrier of essential meaning.

### Signal

The meaningful distinction an artifact is intended to preserve or transmit.

### State

A meaningful condition of a system, component, or interaction that can be
perceived and, when relevant, acted upon.

### Token

A named implementation value representing a design decision. Tokens encode a
specification; they do not replace one.

### Visual force

Any property that directs or resists attention, including scale, contrast,
position, density, direction, color, or motion.

## Required distinctions

| Do not collapse | Distinction |
|---|---|
| Coherence / consistency | Coherence preserves relationships; consistency repeats treatment. |
| Simple / simplistic | Simple preserves necessary meaning; simplistic removes necessary meaning. |
| Minimal / empty | Minimal is sufficient; empty is under-articulated. |
| System / template | A system permits governed variation; a template repeats an arrangement. |
| Primitive / symbol | A primitive is behavior; a symbol is an expression. |
| Evidence / inspiration | Evidence tests a proposition; inspiration suggests a possibility. |
| Rule / habit | A rule protects an invariant; a habit is merely familiar. |
| Ambiguity / confusion | Ambiguity is intentional openness; confusion is failed structure. |
| Calm / inert | Calm is controlled energy; inert lacks directed energy. |
| Precise / sterile | Precision clarifies relationships; sterility removes human consequence. |

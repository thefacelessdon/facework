# FVS-400 — Composition

Composition turns parts into an intelligible whole. Facework compositions should
feel calm but not inert, precise but not sterile, and constructed rather than
decorated.

## Dependency order

Information appears in the order needed to understand what follows. A reader
should not need a later section to decode an earlier one unless the delay is an
intentional narrative device.

The default sequence is:

```text
Orientation → Claim → Structure → Evidence → Implication → Next path
```

Not every artifact requires every stage, but no stage may appear without the
context necessary to interpret it.

## Hierarchy

Use the fewest levels that truthfully represent the content. Hierarchy is formed
through a coordinated change in scale, weight, space, position, and tone—not by
making every heading dramatically different.

Rules:

- one primary focal relationship per bounded view;
- no more than three simultaneous emphasis levels without strong need;
- metadata remains available without competing with the subject;
- visual prominence must track informational consequence;
- repeated elements receive stable treatment.

## Density

Density is a semantic property. Dense fields signal evidence, complexity,
activity, or compression. Open fields signal orientation, gravity, pause, or
uncertainty.

Do not make every screen sparse in pursuit of minimalism. Do not make every
screen dense in pursuit of seriousness. Use density to distinguish layers and
moments.

## Rhythm

Rhythm is established through recurring intervals, line lengths, module sizes,
rules, and pauses. A stable rhythm may be interrupted to communicate a genuine
change in state or importance.

Spacing between unrelated regions must be greater than spacing within a region.
Section rhythm should remain perceivable even when content length varies.

## Composition families

### Reading field

A single dominant column for essays, standards, notes, and long-form evidence.
Side material remains subordinate and may become inline at smaller scales.

### Bilateral field

Two related forces around a shared axis. Use for comparison, negotiation,
before/after, theory/practice, or signal/context—not generic feature grids.

### System field

Modules connected through declared dependencies. Direction, state, and boundary
must be explicit. A system field is a model, not an arrangement of cards.

### Evidence field

Claim and substantiation are kept visibly adjacent. Sources, state, date, and
limitations remain discoverable.

### Index field

Many objects are made navigable through stable metadata, filters, sequence, and
scannable rhythm. Indexes privilege comparison over spectacle.

### Singular field

One statement, object, or transition receives most of the available attention.
Use sparingly at true thresholds, openings, conclusions, or moments of gravity.

## Layering

Visual layers should correspond to conceptual layers:

- **foreground:** present task, claim, or active object;
- **support:** explanation, controls, or adjacent evidence;
- **context:** lineage, system state, metadata, or environment;
- **substrate:** grid, field, or quiet texture that stabilizes the composition.

Decorative layers with no conceptual role are removed.

## Imagery

Imagery is evidence, observation, explanation, or atmosphere with a declared
purpose. Human photography is permitted when people and context are the subject;
it must not reduce culture to generic lifestyle texture.

Preferred image roles include:

- documentary observation;
- diagrams and maps;
- material or architectural evidence;
- primary-source artifacts;
- process and revision;
- restrained abstract fields derived from system behavior.

Captions identify why an image matters, not only what it depicts.

## Anti-patterns

- endless same-weight card grids;
- a decorative hero unrelated to the argument;
- more than one dominant call to action in a bounded view;
- centered paragraph text used as a default;
- visual variety introduced to compensate for weak content structure;
- metadata hidden because it is aesthetically inconvenient;
- whitespace so excessive that relationships become distant or ambiguous;
- dense complexity presented without entry points or landmarks.

# FN-03: Public Records

**Headline finding: the mood-board panel got this wrong twice.**

1. The real UPLOAD #13 still is **grey paper with black halftone and one small
   acid-green label.** It is not an acid-green ground with a red label.
2. The attached `public-records-upload.mp4` **is not UPLOAD #13.** It's a Public
   Records **October 2026 calendar** for 233 Butler St, Brooklyn: an olive-green
   poster where the month's lineup grows as a **network graph of artist names.**

Both are useful, for different reasons.

## 2. THE UPLOAD #13 (still, 6.png)

- **Composition:** two vertical halftone ovals side by side, like two halves of a
  brain or two faces seen from above (my reading). On top, a stack of three
  labels: "THE" (small white tab, tilted about −5°), "UPLOAD" (large acid-green
  tab), "#13" (small white tab, tilted the other way). Lots of empty grey around
  it.
- **Type:** a bitmap or pixel display face, uppercase, black. The same face at
  three sizes. Hierarchy comes from tab size, not weight.
- **Texture:** a coarse halftone dot screen, black on light grey, with the dots
  scaling to make the tone. The labels have soft drop shadows and slightly
  irregular edges, like **real stickers on paper.** The whole frame has a fine
  paper grain.
- **Color (sampled):** ground #E5E5E5; label green #CCE302 / #CBE101 (acid
  yellow-green); type ink near-black. **Only one hue in the whole image.**
- **Emotion:** playful, cheap on purpose, confident. It looks pasted together in
  five minutes, and it's well balanced.

## 3. Public Records October 2026 calendar (the video)

Everything below is from the frames, with times in seconds.

- **Layout:** an olive-green field (#718D46, sampled at 1 s) framed on the right
  and bottom by red-orange bands (#CF3830). On the right, top to bottom: "2026"
  (the digits jitter), a black panel with a white eye or lens glyph, "233",
  "BUTLER ST BROOKLYN NY". Along the bottom: "OCTOBER" in a wide geometric face,
  then a black square with a white "R"-like glyph.
- **Motion 1, kinetic letters:** in "OCTOBER" and "2026", individual letters
  jump up and down between frames. Every 1 s sample shows a different baseline
  arrangement (compare 0 s, 1 s, 2 s, 3 s). The "R" glyph changes shape every
  sample, rotating or morphing between an R, a squiggle and a vertical stroke.
- **Motion 2, the growing network:** the green field starts almost empty at 0 s
  (only "4AM NYC … A Joyful Noise … AADJA … adobeprincess" across the top). From
  1 s to about 16 s, artist names appear one at a time **down and outward along
  thin connecting lines**: Alyssa Stowers, Anastasia Kristensen, Andi, Andy
  Randazzo, AZA, Benét, Blue Hour, Blue Mena, CARISTA, Carlos Niño, and on. By
  about 14 s the field is a dense node-and-line map of the full lineup. It holds
  until about 22 s, and at **23 s the field is empty again** (loop reset).
- **Type:** small grotesk names (two-line stacks) on the network; heavy black
  display type on the red bands.
- **Color:** green + red + black + white. Loud, but with only two hues, held flat.
- **Emotion:** a community being drawn. You watch the venue's month assemble
  itself name by name.

## 4. What it means for Facework

- **"Loud" is one sticker, not a whole ground.** UPLOAD #13 is mostly grey and
  black. The single acid-green tab carries the shout. That's *more* compatible
  with the Reading Room than the mood-board panel suggested: an issue accent can
  live on **one label**, not a whole cover field. This weakens the case for my
  first-pass proposal (an accent-colored cover field) and strengthens a narrower
  one: **one accent label per issue.**
- **The lineup-as-network is the Index, almost literally.** Names appearing one by
  one along connecting lines is exactly how an Are.na channel grows (blocks
  connected to channels). Candidate motion primitive: **accretion**. A channel
  view where blocks arrive one at a time along their connections, then settle.
  It's also the most concrete visual form of Petersen's networks thesis I found
  (see FN-06).
- **Halftone means reproduction.** In UPLOAD it marks the image as a printed
  copy. That backs the first-pass rule: halftone only on captured or reproduced
  material.
- **Avoid copying:** pixel type (it's Public Records' voice), the green-and-red
  pairing (it collides with Facework's settled and exposure status hues), and the
  jittering letters on anything you're meant to read.

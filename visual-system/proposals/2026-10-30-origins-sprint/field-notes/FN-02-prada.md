# FN-02: Prada, "Drawing Prada"

**Headline finding:** there are two layers here, and the mood board merged them.

- **The film** (`prada-pomery.mp4`) is *finished* work: fully painted
  backgrounds and clean color cel animation, with live-action people composited
  in. It's polished, not sketchy.
- **The stills** are where the *process* shows: pencil roughs, red construction
  lines, a storyboard slate, animation-paper peg holes.

The process is shown as a separate layer next to the finished work, not as the
style of the finished work.

## 2. Composition

- **Film:** classic animated-feature staging. Wide establishing shots of a
  painted snowy forest (0:00, 0:07–0:09), then cuts in to product close-ups at
  character scale: dalmatian-print shoes on a painted plank floor with a real hand
  reaching in (0:05–0:06), the bag hanging from a coat hook a cartoon bat is
  holding (0:27–0:30), brogues with red laces being explored by cartoon mice
  (0:32–0:33).
- **Live action and drawing share space and touch each other.** A fox walks beside
  the model in the red dress (0:07–0:08). A lynx makes a snowball (0:22). Mice
  carry a shoe into a mouse hole while the actress peers in (0:35–0:39). In still
  4, the drawn bat *holds the real bag's strap.*
- **Stills 3 and 5:** the photographed model stands in front of **layered paper
  sheets** (a cream top band and a grey lower band, with a visible seam). Pencil
  drawings sit *behind and in front of* her: the cat in still 5 overlaps her feet.
  Still 5 has a penciled slate "Scn 2 (A)" at top right, and **animation peg-bar
  holes** (a black dot and two slots) along the bottom edge.

## 3. Type

- **Opening (0:00–0:01):** "PRADA" in the house wordmark, white, centered over
  the first shot.
- **End card (0:51–0:54):** "Drawing Prada" in a connected **script**, navy on a
  flat pale blue card, three seconds held.
- **In the stills:** hand lettering only ("Scn 2", "A" circled, in pencil).

Type does almost nothing here. Two cards: the brand at the start, the
process-named title at the end.

## 4. Motion

- **Hard cuts between shots, held about 1–3 s each.** Inside a shot it's
  character animation: squash and stretch on the lynx (0:20–0:23), arcing
  follow-through on the fox's tail (0:09). Snow-swirl effects are drawn too
  (0:08, 0:46–0:47).
- **Humans move like live action, characters like animation,** and the film
  never makes one move like the other. That contrast is the point.
- **End:** the title card is static; there's no logo animation.

## 5. Texture and material

- **Film:** painted gouache-style backgrounds (soft snow gradients, painted wood
  grain on floors, firelight bloom at 0:35). Cel characters with inked outlines.
  No pencil roughs visible anywhere in the film.
- **Stills:** graphite line with smudge and erasure; red pencil construction
  lines (the tree in still 3); paper seams and creases; peg holes. This is the
  real material of hand animation.
- **Lighting matched to the drawings.** In still 4 the firelight warms the man's
  left side and the wood floor reflection matches the painted fire. This fits the
  credit that Glen Luchford shot to match the drawings (verify under Web
  research).

The film is cool and white (snow) outdoors and warm and amber indoors by
firelight (0:12–0:14, 0:30–0:35), with one saturated product color per shot (red
dress, blue armchair, red laces). The stills are graphite on warm paper, and the
product supplies the only saturated color: **the red construction pencil and the
red dress share a hue family.** Process color and product color rhyme.

## 7. Emotion

Warm, nostalgic, a little funny. The cartoon animals are cheeky, never cloying.
The stills feel more *serious* than the film: you're looking at the craft
behind it, and that's what gives the film its value. The human hand is the luxury
being sold.

## 8. What it means for Facework

- **Process and finished work are separate layers that point at each other.**
  Prada didn't make the film look sketchy; it published the sketches *next to* the
  film. For Facework: a finished reading stays clean (the Record), and its working
  material (notes, roughs, the notebook) is published *beside* it as a linked
  layer. Don't give the reading a fake pencil texture.
- **Real process marks are vocabulary:** the slate ("Scn 2 (A)") is a label
  system, close to Facework's mono record labels. Peg holes show registration.
  Red construction lines show how something was built. Candidate: **construction
  lines in the issue accent**, used only on the process layer, showing how a
  diagram was derived.
- **Interleaving depth** (a drawing in front of a person, a person in front of a
  drawing) is how two worlds share one frame with dignity. Neither is pasted on top
  of the other.
- **Avoid copying:** cartoon characters (Facework's narrator is a lens, never a
  character, and this is the exact trap), script end cards, and fashion
  photography as a register.

# FN-05: John Maeda

**Headline finding:** the verifiable Maeda lesson is **constraint**, not
pastel geometry. Design by Numbers allowed one tone scale (0–100), no color, and a
handful of commands, and that's what produced its calm. The mood-board panel's
pastel spheres and cones are a generated summary: **I found no source tying that
look to Maeda.**

## 1. What I opened (via research agent; URLs checked)

- ACG page written by Maeda, 6 May 1999: http://acg.media.mit.edu/events/adc/ACGatADC/index.html
  - He founded the Aesthetics and Computation Group in 1996 to stop separating
    art/design from engineering.
  - In computation, a program "explicitly instructs the canvas on where and how to
    apply virtual pigments."
- DBN introduction: http://dbn.media.mit.edu/introduction.html
  - `Paper 50` gives a grey ground, `Paper 100` is black.
  - Coordinates and tone both run 0–100.
  - `Line 0 0 100 100` draws a corner-to-corner line.
- DBN vs Processing comparison: https://people.csail.mit.edu/bmcutler/gaudi/gaudi_from_simon/processing-0068h03/reference/compare/dbn.html (DBN has no color)
- DBN purpose, to get "more non-programmers to program": https://maedastudio.com/1999/dbn/index.php
- Laws of Simplicity, ten laws: http://lawsofsimplicity.com/. Law 10, "The One": "subtracting the obvious and adding the meaningful."
- Design in Tech 2018: https://www.slideshare.net/johnmaeda/design-in-tech-report-2018 (Classical / Design Thinking / Computational design)
- **Not opened:** a Quartz piece on computational design (403) and a Project MUSE DBN review (blocked). The "100×100 pixel canvas" figure appears only in search snippets, so it's **unverified**.
- **Not found:** Maeda statements about grids or monochrome as an explicit
  aesthetic. Monochrome is supported only as DBN's grayscale constraint.

## 2–6. Composition, type, motion, texture, color

**Not observed firsthand.** I viewed no Maeda artwork in this sprint. The
documented facts: DBN is grayscale only (0–100) on a square coordinate field;
forms come from lines and loops.

## 7. Emotion

Not observed. The documented intent is to teach and include non-programmers, so
calm comes from a small, learnable rule set.

## 8. What it means for Facework

- **Calm comes from a small rule set.** The Reading Room's 4pt scale, one accent
  and three voices already are this kind of DBN constraint. Maeda supports
  *keeping* the constraints tight, not adding a pastel palette.
- **Code as material = instruments drawn from data.** Facework's five instruments
  (DESIGN.md §7) are generated from records. That's the computational-design
  stance, already canon.
- **"Subtract the obvious, add the meaningful"** is a good review test for every
  first-pass proposal. It argues against the halftone and notebook textures
  unless they carry meaning (see FN-03, FN-04).
- **Drop from the first pass:** "pastel and ink on white" and "soft geometric
  forms" as Maeda-derived. No source supports them.

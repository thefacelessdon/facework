# FN-01: The New York Times at 175

**Headline finding:** in the attached film, the wordmark holds dead still while
everything around it flickers. Reporters' notes, archive photos, contact sheets
and classified folders swap almost every quarter-second, and thin orange
selection boxes jump between them. *The institution holds; the evidence moves.*
That is the most transferable idea in the whole source set.

## 2. Composition (film)

- **Three fixed bands in a 3:4 portrait frame.** At every frame I checked
  (0.5 s, 6.0 s, 11.9 s): an upper collage, the wordmark centered at about 48%
  height on a clear white strip, and a lower collage.
- **The wordmark never moves.** Its position, size and color are identical in all
  48 sampled frames. Everything else is arranged around it.
- **Collage pieces bleed off every edge.** Headlines are cropped ("…l Fires" at
  top left, "he Teller" at lower left, a huge black "TH… / KIDS…" at bottom
  right). Nothing is shown whole: you're looking at fragments of the record.
- **Recurring pieces** (all frames): a construction-worker photo at top right; a
  35mm contact strip with frame numbers 14–15A, tilted about 5°; a black-and-white
  Apollo image at top left (it alternates between the lunar module and astronauts
  with the flag, e.g. 0.5 s vs 11.9 s); the Pentagon Papers cover
  "SECRET – SENSITIVE / U.S. – VIETNAM RELATIONS / 1945 – 1967" at lower left;
  a "Portraits of Grief" tear sheet at lower right.
- **Where the eye goes:** the orange box decides it, not the layout.

## 3. Type

- **Wordmark:** NYT Fraktur (Benguiat), black, on clear white. Brand Identity
  confirms the face and credits Matthew Carter for NYT Cheltenham and Franklin.
- **Headline fragments are real Times typography:** a condensed bold serif for
  "…l Fires", a light grey serif for "he Teller" (sampled #7E7E7E at 0.5 s), and
  a heavy slab black for "TH…". I read these as Cheltenham weights. That's
  inference; nothing on screen labels them.
- **Handwriting is a type layer.** Blue ballpoint reporter notes, transcribed
  across frames:
  - "11/28/2022" with a red X-in-circle mark (0.5 s)
  - "10/21/22 Grand Rapids" (around 0.75 s, 3.25 s, 5.5 s)
  - "10/17/22 Worthington" (around 1.25 s)
  - "South Ridge → came w/ 15, then turned 16" (around 1.5–2.0 s, 11.9 s)
  - "Albertville, Zaira" (around 0 s, 2.5 s)
  - "Palm Beach… Homestead" (around 0.5 s, 3.0 s)
  - "First day in Enterprise" (around 6.0 s)
  - "…Idaho… his brake fire… 23 December" (around 12 s)
  
  Partial words are my best reading.
- **T175 still (2.jpg):** the mark is the Times "T" in Fraktur plus "175" in
  heavy blackletter-derived numerals. The "1" has a diamond foot serif and a
  flagged head, the "7" ends in a long calligraphic leg, and the "5" closes on a
  tapered arc. This matches the Brand Identity description: 7 as "a calligraphic
  flick" from the k's leg, 5 as a "tapered arc." The mark ink samples #1A1A1A
  (near-black, not pure black).

## 4. Motion (film)

- **Cuts, not eases.** Collage pieces change on hard cuts at roughly 4–8 swaps per
  second. Consecutive 0.25 s samples almost always show a different note, photo or
  folder state. Nothing slides or fades in any frame I checked.
- **The orange selection box** is a thin 1–2px rectangle. It *jumps* to a new
  position and size every frame or two (in sampled frames it sits on the notes,
  the folder, the "Teller" headline and empty white). It reads like an editor's
  crop mark or a reporter's eye checking one item after another.
- **What holds still:** the wordmark, the frame structure and the large headline
  fragments. **What moves:** evidence (notes, photos, folder covers, contact-strip
  frames). The folder flips between the green Pentagon Papers cover, white pages
  (around 2.5 s and 8.5 s) and a grey divider tab "VIET POLITICAL MOVEMENTS
  1947–1950" (11.9 s).
- **It loops:** the 12.18 s clip ends on a composition close to its opening.
- **Picture and sound run on different clocks:** the collage cuts at 4–8 per
  second, while the sound repeats on a steady 2.5 s cycle (second pass). The fast
  evidence sits over a slow, regular, machine-like pulse. That's a candidate
  for Facework sound, if it ever needs any: a slow, steady pulse under fast cuts,
  never a fast pulse matched to the cuts.
- **The mark's own motion:** Brand Identity says XYZ Type and Keegan had "the
  same brief" and worked as a team, so the mark and its motion were designed
  together. That's a paraphrase, not a quote. I could not see it.

## 5. Texture and material

- **The ground is clean white** (#FDFDFD at 0.5 s), not cream. All the age and
  warmth comes from the *objects*: yellowed tear sheet, fluorescent-lit folder,
  scanned contact strip with sprocket holes, ballpoint on paper.
- The T175 still uses archive as a ground: a real front page faded to about 10%
  contrast (#FAFAFA paper sample), with the mark in full ink on top.
- No halftone, grain overlay or noise filter is added. The material is *real
  scanned stuff*, shown flat.

## 7. Emotion

Urgent but steady. The flicker carries the work: a lot of reporting, fast, across
decades and places. The motionless wordmark carries the trust. It never feels
chaotic, because the one thing that should stay still does. The handwriting makes
it human; the orange box makes it feel *checked*.

## 8. What it means for Facework

- **Quiet confidence = one element that never moves.** For Facework that's the
  Coherence Mark and wordmark. The Index (blocks, notes, sources) is what moves
  around it. This maps directly onto the Reading Room's governing idea: the
  Record holds, the Field moves.
- **Visible process = show the real artifacts flat.** Notes, scans and contact
  sheets, shown as themselves. No fake grain. The Times adds no texture; the
  objects already have it.
- **One accent that points.** The orange box is the closest analogue to how
  verdigris is meant to work: it marks attention, never decorates. Candidate
  motion primitive: **the attention box**, a 1px verdigris rectangle that jumps
  (hard cut) to the thing being read.
- **Mark and wordmark in one family.** The numerals borrow strokes from the
  wordmark letters ("a family resemblance, not a logo placed next to a logo",
  Brattengeier). Facework's equivalent: any issue numeral or nameplate is built
  from the Register wordmark's own strokes, not taken from a separate blackletter
  font.
- **Avoid copying:** blackletter itself (it's the Times' 175-year inheritance, not
  ours), the Pentagon Papers and 9/11 tear sheets (other people's history), and
  the specific orange.

The mark holds; the record moves. One thin accent points at what’s being read.

That is what the New York Times 175 film shows across all 48 frames I sampled, and it's the idea this system is built on. The board is rebuilt from seven field notes, not from the mood board, which turned out to misdescribe four of its seven sources. It extends the locked identity (Coherence Mark, Register wordmark, Literata / Schibsted Grotesk / Spline Sans Mono, verdigris). Every rule cites a field note in field-notes/.

Deliverable 4 · motion principles · 05-motion-principles.md

The mark holds; the record moves

P1 · HoldThe lockup never takes part in content motionIn the full Times film, the 175 lockup cuts on complete at about 1:11 and holds. No draw-in. Proposed: keep Facework’s draw-in for the identity moment only.
P2 · Two clocksEvidence cuts; state changes settleEvidence swaps instantly at 120–250 ms per item. Open→issued uses --rr-ease-settle. FN-01 hard cuts.
P3 · Attention boxA 1px verdigris rectangle that jumps to what’s being readNever tweened, one per screen. FN-01 orange box.
P4 · AccretionChannels build block by block along their connections, then settleOnce on the web; may loop in social. FN-03 calendar network.
P5 · Two worldsArchive moves like a document; Facework drawings draw inNo parallax or animation of real people’s archival images. FN-02 contrast.
P6 · Parallel trackProcess is shown beside the work, never as a filter on itNo fake boil, grain or wobble. FN-02 stills vs film.

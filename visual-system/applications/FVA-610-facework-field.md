# FVA-610 — Facework Field

## Proposition

Facework Field is a weekly reflective ritual that makes identity dynamics
visible through a generated Lorenz trajectory. A participant answers three
stable prompts, sees those inputs translated into a bounded pattern, and may
preserve the result in a local archive.

> Small acts shape the pattern.

The experience is an experiment within the public Facework knowledge system. It
does not replace the face.works homepage, the Coherence Mark, or the product
interfaces defined elsewhere in FVS-900.

## Epistemic boundary

The generated form is a **reflective representation**, not a psychological
assessment, biometric signature, identity score, or scientific inference about
the participant. Three self-reported inputs modulate a declared mathematical
system. The result may prompt interpretation; it cannot validate one.

The Lorenz attractor is an application-specific visual instrument. Axis Exchange
remains the canonical Facework identity mark. Their bilateral resemblance does
not make them interchangeable.

## Core ritual

1. **Enter** — choose private tracing or inspect the collective-field concept.
2. **Reflect** — answer three timeless prompts, one at a time.
3. **Generate** — see the declared mapping become a bounded trajectory.
4. **Name** — add an optional one-line Face Note.
5. **Preserve** — save the trace and its inputs to the local archive.
6. **Return** — repeat no more than weekly by default and compare change over
   time without streaks, scores, or coercive reminders.

## The three prompts

### Embodied presence

**How present were you with other people this week?**

A direct 0–100 response modulates the attractor's initial spread and `rho`
within a bounded operating range.

### Digital expression

**How did you choose to appear online this week?**

The reference choices are Reserved, Instrumental, Expressive, and Porous. The
choice changes a declared analytic line color and the initial condition. It does
not assign a good/bad hierarchy.

### Infrastructural stability

**How well did the systems supporting you hold this week?**

A direct 0–100 response modulates `beta`, line persistence, and local motion
stability within tested bounds.

The words are primary; the numeric controls make the mapping honest. Voice may
be explored later, but is excluded from v0.1 because dependable local-only
transcription cannot yet be treated as a universal browser capability.

## Parameter contract

The reference fixes the Lorenz family and varies only bounded values:

| Input | Visible effect | Mathematical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Embodied presence | trajectory spread | `rho` 24–32; `x₀` offset |
| Digital expression | declared analytic hue | four fixed hues; `y₀` offset |
| System stability | persistence and steadiness | `beta` 2.2–3.2; trail alpha |
| Weekly seed | non-repeating detail | small deterministic `z₀` perturbation |

`sigma` remains 10 in the reference. The interface exposes the mapping after
generation. No hidden sentiment model or inferred score is used.

## Single-player v0.1

- no account;
- no network request;
- no analytics;
- no microphone or camera;
- local browser storage only after explicit save;
- delete-one and clear-all controls;
- downloadable image export deferred until export metadata can remain attached.

## Collective Field

The included collective view is a **simulation** using deterministic synthetic
seeds. It demonstrates spatial and motion behavior; it contains no participant
data and uploads nothing.

A real multiplayer release requires an independent privacy and community-safety
gate covering informed consent, data minimization, deletion, retention,
moderation of Face Notes, abuse response, threat modeling, aggregation
thresholds, and an accessible structured alternative. “Anonymous” may not be
claimed solely because a username is absent.

## Visual translation

- Field mode is appropriate because the primary object is a time-based system.
- Structural mono carries prompts, values, mappings, and privacy state.
- The neutral sans carries reflection and explanation.
- Generated trajectories use one analytic hue at a time; FVS semantic state
  colors remain reserved for system meaning.
- Axis Exchange identifies Facework at the threshold but never becomes a trace.
- The canvas is the field; controls remain ordinary, visible, and inspectable.

## Motion

The trajectory reveals sensitivity and continuity. It does not flap, breathe,
or celebrate. The generation sequence may draw for up to 900 milliseconds after
the last response; controls remain available throughout.

Reduced motion renders a complete static trajectory immediately. The collective
simulation becomes a static constellation. No information depends on motion.

## Responsive transformation

Desktop holds the canvas and ritual panel in a bilateral field. Mobile places
the prompt before the visualization, uses a compact static or low-particle
canvas, and retains every action with 44-pixel minimum targets. Hover-only trace
inspection is prohibited; tap and keyboard selection remain available.

## Release gates

- prompt comprehension testing across different life contexts;
- confirmation that repeated use creates reflection rather than self-scoring;
- performance testing on representative low-power mobile devices;
- real-device touch, screen-reader, and reduced-motion testing;
- privacy, moderation, deletion, and aggregation review before multiplayer;
- scientific-language review before any public claims about chaos or identity.

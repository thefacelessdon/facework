# FVS-900 — Applications

Applications translate the same visual intelligence into different media. This
document defines what each artifact class must preserve and what it may vary.

## Universal application requirements

Every canonical application includes:

- a declared purpose and audience;
- the relevant FVS derivation;
- accessible and medium-appropriate structure;
- version, status, and ownership metadata;
- known constraints and intentional exceptions;
- evaluation against FVS-000;
- a durable source or export where appropriate.

## Identity

### Coherence Mark

The mark must derive from meaningful relationships—currently hypothesized as an
axis, bilateral forces, tension, convergence, expansion, and continuity. It must
not be approved solely because it resembles a butterfly or looks balanced.

Required tests:

- recognizable in positive and negative form;
- retains structural identity at small scale;
- functions without color or motion;
- can be constructed from documented geometry;
- remains culturally and contextually appropriate;
- is distinguishable from generic butterfly, neural, wellness, and AI marks.

The canonical derivation and production assets are recorded in
[FVI-001 — Coherence Mark Derivation](identity/FVI-001-coherence-mark-derivation.md).
The mark remains a reference implementation, not a constitutional primitive.

### Logotype

The logotype preserves canonical naming: **Facework** for the practice and
**face.works** for the domain. Customization must improve identity or optical
function; arbitrary glyph modification is prohibited.

## face.works

The public site is an interface to Facework's knowledge architecture, not a
marketing shell around the protocol.

Human-facing navigation may use:

- Field Notes;
- Models;
- Frameworks;
- Cases;
- Conversations;
- Experiments;
- Library;
- About.

Underneath, artifacts retain canonical relationships to Theory, Discipline,
Practice, Implementation, Evidence, and Lineage. The public experience reveals
that architecture progressively rather than exposing repository taxonomy.

The homepage behaves as a public record of attention: what Facework is noticing,
connecting, testing, and learning. It should feel curated and alive, never
careless or continuously animated.

## Documents and standards

- Prefer durable text-first source formats.
- Use visible identifiers, version, status, and lineage.
- Optimize long-form reading before graphic display.
- Tables carry exact mappings; diagrams carry relationships; prose carries
  argument.
- Exports preserve selectable text, links, headings, reading order, and metadata.
- A document must remain useful in monochrome and when printed.

## Diagrams

Every diagram declares:

1. the question it answers;
2. the entities shown;
3. the meaning of connection, position, direction, size, and color;
4. the states or time represented;
5. what has been omitted;
6. an accessible text description.

Protocol concepts do not receive arbitrary icons. Repeated symbols must be
registered with definitions and tested for distinguishability.

## Presentations

- One governing idea per slide or deliberate multi-panel comparison.
- Slides support spoken argument; they do not duplicate a document page.
- Sources and status remain present.
- Type and diagrams are sized for the actual room or device.
- Motion is limited to explaining sequence, state, or transformation.
- A static export preserves the argument.

## Field Notes and social artifacts

Field Notes preserve the immediacy of observation without pretending every idea
is settled. They include date and status and may expose links to the models or
evidence they later influence.

Social derivatives are excerpts or gateways, not flattened substitutes for the
source artifact. The platform may change crop, length, and density; it may not
change the claim or erase provenance.

## Cases and evidence

Case materials show structural change, not only polished deliverables. They
identify:

- the original incoherence or constraint;
- the intervention;
- the resulting system;
- evidence and limitations;
- ownership, consent, and confidentiality boundaries.

## Physical artifacts

Books, cards, posters, apparel, signage, and environments derive from the same
relationships. Material, production, scale, tactility, and lifespan become part
of the derivation. A mark placed on an object does not make the object Facework.

## Reference implementation policy

An implementation becomes canonical only after:

1. documented derivation;
2. evaluation in its intended context;
3. accessibility and technical verification;
4. review against relevant Atlas findings;
5. addition to the artifact registry with a version and owner.

Canonical implementations may be superseded. Their lineage remains preserved.

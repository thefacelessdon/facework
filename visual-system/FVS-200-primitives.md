# FVS-200 — Visual Primitives

Visual primitives are irreducible behaviors. They describe how relationships
become perceptible before any decision about motif, component, or medium.

## 1. Axis

**Definition:** The organizing relationship around which a system forms.

An axis may be visible or implied, vertical or horizontal, central or offset,
spatial or temporal. Every composition must be able to name its primary axis.

**Valid expressions:** baseline, reading edge, timeline, centerline, sequence,
shared datum, or stable alignment.

**Failure:** several competing axes with no declared hierarchy.

**Test:** What holds this composition together?

## 2. Balance

**Definition:** The intelligible distribution of visual forces.

Balance is not symmetry. It may be achieved through counterweight, interval,
density, scale, silence, repetition, or controlled asymmetry.

**Valid expressions:** equal bilateral force, heavy/light counterweight, a dense
field balanced by open space, or a dominant object stabilized by a quiet edge.

**Failure:** equilibrium produced by centering everything or making every part
equally loud.

**Test:** What counters the dominant force?

## 3. Tension

**Definition:** Productive difference between elements, forces, or states.

Tension makes change and consequence visible. Contrast, compression, distance,
misalignment, boundary, and interruption may create tension.

**Valid expressions:** before/after, signal/noise, stable/unstable, field/boundary,
or compression/release.

**Failure:** contrast with no semantic purpose or friction that blocks use.

**Test:** What difference is this artifact asking the audience to notice?

## 4. Flow

**Definition:** Designed movement of attention, information, or action.

Flow operates through spatial sequence, reading order, interaction, and time.
It must provide orientation as well as movement.

**Valid expressions:** dependency order, progressive disclosure, directional
line, narrative sequence, transition, or task path.

**Failure:** motion without destination, novelty scroll behavior, or a hierarchy
that sends attention in conflicting directions.

**Test:** Where does attention enter, travel, pause, and leave?

## 5. Module

**Definition:** A repeatable unit capable of participating in a larger structure.

A module has defined anatomy, behavior, boundary, and connection rules. It can
vary without losing its role.

**Valid expressions:** note, citation, node, panel, diagram unit, metadata block,
or content record.

**Failure:** cardification—placing unrelated content in identical containers
without structural reason.

**Test:** What relationship allows this unit to combine with others?

## 6. Scale

**Definition:** Preservation of essential relationships across changes in size,
density, audience, or context.

Scale is adaptive, not literal. Details may disappear, combine, or transform so
that the governing relationship remains perceivable.

**Valid expressions:** responsive hierarchy, optical simplification, nested
systems, or alternate diagram resolution.

**Failure:** shrinking a large composition until it becomes technically present
but practically illegible.

**Test:** What must survive when the artifact becomes smaller, larger, denser,
or more distant?

## 7. Continuity

**Definition:** Persistence of identity and intelligible relationship through
variation and change.

Continuity emerges from stable behaviors: axis, interval, hierarchy, state,
motion logic, language, and lineage.

**Valid expressions:** a familiar reading rhythm across formats, persistent
semantic state, shared diagram grammar, or motion that preserves spatial origin.

**Failure:** reliance on logo placement, identical layouts, or decorative motifs
as the sole source of recognition.

**Test:** What remains true when the surface treatment changes?

## Primitive combinations

Most artifacts use several primitives. The combination must be explainable.

| Pattern | Primitive combination | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Bilateral field | Axis + Balance + Tension | Show related forces around a shared center |
| Knowledge path | Axis + Flow + Continuity | Connect observation to durable knowledge |
| System map | Module + Flow + Scale | Reveal parts, connections, and levels |
| State transition | Tension + Flow + Continuity | Make change legible without disorientation |
| Editorial spread | Axis + Balance + Scale | Preserve reading hierarchy across formats |

## Relationship to protocol primitives

Protocol primitives define what Facework does. Visual primitives define how a
relationship can become perceptible. A protocol primitive may call upon several
visual primitives:

| Protocol concept | Likely visual behavior |
|---|---|
| Semantics | Tension isolates signal; Axis establishes reading order |
| Field | Module identifies actors; Balance and Tension show forces |
| Taste | Tension establishes thresholds; Continuity preserves the quality bar |
| Frequency | Axis establishes recurrence; Flow gives rhythm direction |
| Current | Flow makes direction and consequence visible |
| Stability | Axis, Balance, and Module establish dependable structure |
| Resonance | Scale and Continuity reveal amplification without identity loss |
| Entropy | Tension reveals structural degradation against a known baseline |
| Sovereignty | Axis and Module clarify boundary, ownership, and connection |
| Consonance | Balance and Continuity show alignment across layers |
| Coherence | All primitives operate as an intelligible whole |

This table is a starting interpretation, not a fixed icon assignment.

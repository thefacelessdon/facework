# FVS-100 — Facework Visual Constitution

## Preamble

The Facework Visual System exists to make coherence perceptible. Visual
communication reveals relationships, directs attention, creates meaning, and
preserves knowledge. It shall be governed by enduring principles rather than
fashion, novelty, or unexplained preference.

## Article I — Meaning

1. Meaning precedes expression. Form emerges from what an artifact must
   communicate, enable, or preserve.
2. Attention is consequential. Emphasis indicates consequence, not merely
   visual interest.
3. Form carries an argument. Placement, scale, proximity, sequence, contrast,
   and movement are acts of interpretation.

## Article II — Structure

1. Visual communication reveals what belongs, differs, depends, precedes, and
   matters.
2. Structure precedes surface. Color, texture, imagery, and ornament reinforce
   structure; they do not substitute for it.
3. Hidden structure becomes visible when doing so materially increases
   understanding.

## Article III — Coherence

1. Every consequential part participates in the whole.
2. Coherence is relational and does not require uniformity.
3. Local optimization must not damage systemic integrity.
4. Contradiction is valid only when it intentionally reveals conflict, change,
   uncertainty, or tension.

## Article IV — Necessity

1. Every element earns its presence by communicating, orienting, distinguishing,
   enabling, or deepening experience.
2. Reduction preserves meaning; absence is not automatically virtue.
3. Decoration is permitted when it participates in meaning, atmosphere, memory,
   identity, or humanity.

## Article V — Legibility

1. Meaning must be perceivable by the intended audience.
2. Hierarchy reflects informational or functional consequence.
3. Complexity receives paths, layers, landmarks, and boundaries.
4. Ambiguity is intentional or it is a defect.

## Article VI — Relationship

1. Relationships precede objects. Facework is recognized through proportion,
   counterbalance, tension, rhythm, sequence, and transformation.
2. Every force has context: emphasis requires quiet, density requires space,
   and movement requires orientation.
3. Unoccupied space is active structure, never merely leftover area.

## Article VII — Continuity

1. Identity survives changes in medium, scale, format, technology, audience,
   authorship, and time.
2. Continuity preserves governing relationships; it is not repetition.
3. Canonical change records what changed, why, what remains, and what it
   supersedes.

## Article VIII — Time

1. Every artifact is encountered through sequence, duration, and attention.
2. Motion reveals cause, continuity, hierarchy, spatial relation,
   transformation, or state.
3. Pace corresponds to meaning. Timing is semantic.

## Article IX — Scale

1. Essential relationships survive scale.
2. Reduction may require transformation rather than literal shrinking.
3. Relationships may recur across levels when recurrence increases
   intelligibility.

## Article X — Modularity

1. Canonical artifacts emerge from reusable relationships, rules, components,
   and patterns.
2. Repeated use alone does not make a module canonical.
3. A valid grammar permits difference; a system incapable of variation is a
   template.

## Article XI — Traceability

1. Consequential decisions are explainable.
2. Canonical artifacts preserve principles, primitives, specifications,
   references, constraints, versions, and history.
3. Authority follows evidence, not authorship, status, preference, or frequency.

## Article XII — Accessibility

1. Coherence must be available across differences in vision, hearing, movement,
   cognition, language, technology, and bandwidth.
2. Essential meaning should not depend on a single signal.
3. Accessible expressions are complete implementations, not secondary versions.

## Article XIII — Humanity

1. Precision shall not confuse sterility with clarity.
2. Artifacts may reveal observation, authorship, process, revision, and time.
3. People, cultures, and communities are not decorative material or
   interchangeable data.

## Article XIV — Durability

1. Knowledge outlives its interface.
2. Canonical work uses durable formats and documented structures where possible.
3. Novelty alone is insufficient justification for change.

> Build things that outlive the tools used to create them.

## Article XV — Amendment

Constitutional change requires:

1. evidence from multiple implementations;
2. a demonstrated limitation or contradiction;
3. analysis of systemic consequences;
4. a list of affected specifications and artifacts;
5. a transition plan preserving lineage;
6. formal adoption in a new constitutional version.

Changes confined to geometry, typography, color, components, or applications
belong in their respective specifications.

## Closing principle

Facework is not defined by a fixed collection of visual objects. It is defined
by a coherent way of forming relationships.

> The goal is not to impose order upon everything. The goal is to make the
> order already present perceptible.

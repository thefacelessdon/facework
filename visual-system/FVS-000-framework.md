# FVS-000 — Facework Visual System Framework

## Purpose

The Facework Visual System defines how Facework makes knowledge visible.

It is not a collection of stylistic preferences. It is a specification for
producing coherent visual artifacts across formats, technologies, contexts, and
generations.

> Visual identity is coherent behavior made visible.

## Mandate

FVS exists to:

1. reveal structure;
2. reduce ambiguity;
3. preserve relationships;
4. make complexity navigable;
5. maintain continuity across artifacts;
6. document the origin of visual decisions;
7. allow expression to evolve without losing identity.

The system does not prescribe one fixed appearance. It establishes the laws
from which appropriate appearances can be derived.

## Invariants and expressions

**Invariants** are relationships that must survive every implementation:
meaning before form, hierarchy that reflects consequence, semantic use of
state, legibility, traceability, continuity, and access.

**Expressions** are variable choices shaped by context: exact scale, density,
palette mode, type implementation, component arrangement, and medium.

Facework's identity resides in its invariants, not in any single expression.

## Visual stack

Every consequential decision belongs to a layer:

```text
Meaning
  ↓
Principle
  ↓
Primitive
  ↓
Geometry
  ↓
Component
  ↓
Composition
  ↓
Behavior
  ↓
Artifact
```

Each layer depends on the one above it. An artifact that cannot be traced
through the stack is visually arbitrary.

## Coherence Design lifecycle

### Observe

Identify meaning, audience, context, constraints, and structural forces.

### Model

Express the relevant relationships independently of appearance.

### Specify

Translate those relationships into primitives, geometry, and rules.

### Compose

Assemble components in accordance with the specification.

### Implement

Produce the artifact within the capabilities and constraints of its medium.

### Evaluate

Test clarity, coherence, continuity, accessibility, and durability.

### Preserve

Record lineage, decisions, version, dependencies, exceptions, and evidence.

The lifecycle is recursive. Evidence from an implementation can refine the
system, but it cannot silently rewrite it.

## Derivation requirement

Every consequential visual decision must answer:

1. What meaning is being expressed?
2. Which constitutional principle governs it?
3. Which primitive makes the relationship visible?
4. Which geometric or compositional rule gives it form?
5. What evidence suggests the result increases coherence?

Taste may guide exploration. Taste alone cannot authorize a canonical decision.

## Canonical evaluation

| Dimension | Required question |
|---|---|
| Meaning | What is this artifact trying to make visible? |
| Structure | Can its essential relationships be perceived? |
| Coherence | Does every consequential decision reinforce the whole? |
| Necessity | Has every element earned its presence? |
| Legibility | Can the intended audience understand and navigate it? |
| Continuity | Does it behave like Facework without relying on the mark? |
| Traceability | Can decisions be derived from the system? |
| Accessibility | Does meaning survive human and technical differences? |
| Durability | Can the knowledge survive this implementation? |

A severe failure in one dimension cannot be concealed by strength in another.

## Evolution

Changes occur at the lowest appropriate layer. A new application may introduce
a component. Repeated component behavior may justify a compositional pattern.
Repeated patterns, supported by evidence across contexts, may reveal a primitive.
Only durable cross-context evidence can justify a constitutional amendment.

> Ideas should earn permanence.

## Relationship to the Facework Protocol

Facework Protocol primitives describe operations within the practice:
Semantics, Field, Taste, Frequency, Current, Flow, Stability, Resonance, Entropy,
Sovereignty, Consonance, and Coherence.

FVS primitives describe visual behavior: Axis, Balance, Tension, Flow, Module,
Scale, and Continuity. The two systems may map to one another, but they are not
interchangeable. FVS-200 defines the mapping rules.

## Foundational commitments

- Structure before decoration.
- Relationships before objects.
- Meaning before expression.
- Legibility before novelty.
- Systems before signatures.
- Evidence before authority.
- Artifacts over interfaces.
- Continuity over consistency.
- Knowledge over software.
- Coherence over style.

The system succeeds when Facework artifacts can vary radically in medium,
scale, audience, and technology while remaining governed by the same underlying
intelligence.

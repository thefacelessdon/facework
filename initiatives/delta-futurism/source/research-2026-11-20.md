# Research notes, Nov 20, 2026

Five research passes fed the v3 story and proposal. Every figure used on the pages is cited in `sources.html`. This file records what was **corrected**, what is **unverified**, and what was **left out**, so nobody reintroduces it.

## Corrections made

- "Six to seven for a dollar" was a misreading. Lange's caption gives hours: hoers near Clarksdale worked 6 a.m. to 7 p.m. for $1 (1937).
- The 1938 Pine Bluff truck photo is by Russell Lee (cotton pickers boarding for the fields), not Lange.
- The Walkers of Belzoni were Cube Walker's family, a tenant-purchase client, photographed by Marion Post Wolcott in Nov 1939. The captions don't say "bought a farm".
- O. W. Gurley was born in Huntsville, Alabama, and raised in Jefferson County: "raised in Pine Bluff".
- The DRA covers 255 counties since WRDA 2024 (252 before). Shelby County (Memphis) and Pulaski County (Little Rock) are inside it.
- Arkansas Cypress goes into service in **December 2028** per the APSC's GAJA report and Entergy's rider. The cost is $1,601.5M (the Jefferson Power Station is also about $1.6B; don't confuse the two).
- Little Rock's current Juneteenth festival dates from about 2009. The stronger true claims: emancipation celebrations in Little Rock, Pine Bluff and Helena by the 1880s, and a 1942 Little Rock union contract that made Juneteenth a paid holiday.

## Unverified: keep off the page until sourced

- "So goes Pine Bluff, so goes the Delta": not found in any source.
- "Negro Paradise of the South": only a 2018 blog. The page uses the 2020 book's wording, "the African American Paradise of the South", attributed to local historians.
- A Barraque Street Black business district: not sourced. The documented district is Main Street, 1880–1925.
- Pine Bluff's Jack Rabbit Club, Rainbow Club and Masonic Temple as Chitlin' Circuit venues: weakly sourced. Needs a Pine Bluff Commercial archive pass.
- YAMS (as an R&B event) and Lemon Pepper Wet (as a touring party): not as described. Ask Stedmon for links.
- Arkansas Cypress's customer net benefit, levelized cost and annual MWh are redacted in the filings.
- Google's total payments to Entergy are disputed: $526M plus $190M for transmission (Democrat-Gazette) vs about $2.1B over 20 years (Entergy).

## Left out on purpose

- xAI Southaven (more than $20B) and AWS Madison County ($21B). They are in the DRA footprint, but in the Memphis and Jackson suburbs, so they're noted, not counted.
- South Arkansas lithium: outside the Delta.
- Slim Chickens: Fayetteville, in the Ozarks, not the Delta. Don't stretch the map.
- Any family's acreage, lease terms or how land is being split. Private; stays in berdia-trust.

## The honest framing the data supports

- **Investment** is real: about $21.9B in the cultural Delta since 2024 ($16.5B Arkansas, $5.4B Mississippi).
- **People** are not yet coming back to the rural Delta. Black population growth in the region is landing in DeSoto (+25.9%) and Madison/Rankin (+10.5%). The Mississippi Delta outside DeSoto is −9.6% and the Arkansas Delta −3.0% (2020–25). Mississippi County, Arkansas has about $7B of steel, about half its steelworkers commute in, and it lost 7% of its people.
- **Capture:** there is no clean public measure for Arkansas or Mississippi projects. Any local-share figure is labelled illustrative.

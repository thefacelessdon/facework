# Delta Futurism: story research and recommended scroll architecture (2026-11-11)

For: the owner, and the parallel session rewriting the copy.
Scope: the Delta view of `delta-futurism.html` (working copy 2026-11-11). The Arkansas Cypress view follows the same rules and is covered briefly at the end.
Outside theorists: none appear in the recommendation. Everything below is about structure, pacing and photographs.

---

## The answer first

**Make the page one walk taken three times.** Choose six places: the river, the road, the field, the home, the gathering and the work. The past walks through them in black and white. The present stops at one of them, half-developed. The future walks the same six places again, in the same order, at eye level, with Delta people in every frame. Each future frame develops to color only when its caption says who owns it.

That fixes the cohesion problem three ways:

1. **Every image has a job.** Each photograph is one of the six places at one time. A photo that is not one of the six places, and is not the opening or closing frame, leaves the Delta view.
2. **Past and future rhyme without sitting side by side.** The reader sees the steamer Capitol as Plate 01 in the past. Later, Plate 01 comes back as the river port. The rhyme comes from the shared number and place, not from a pair layout.
3. **The darkroom effect becomes the argument, not decoration.** Today the images develop because you scrolled. In the new version, an image reaches full color at the line where the caption names the local owner. Color means ownership. That is the page's thesis ("capability, agency and ownership remains"), carried by the images.

On the owner's open question about pairs: **don't place past and future side by side.** Pairs read as a before-and-after slider. They imply "then was bad, later is good." They also put AI concept images right next to real 1937 negatives, where the concepts look weakest and least honest. Make the rhyme across distance instead: the same plate numbers, the same six places, the same crop logic.

The biggest single problem the research found is not layout. **The past is people at eye level (Lange, Wolcott). The future, as published, is machines seen from above (the `r-*` aerials and product renders).** The page changes subject halfway through: it starts about people and ends about equipment. The round-1 future images (`future-images-round1/20–27`) are already people at eye level, and most of them already rhyme with a specific FSA frame. Use them, or their regenerated versions, and retire the aerial and product renders from the Delta view.

## Revision (same day): the present is the moment, not a pause between past and future

The first draft gave the present one photograph. That undersold it. The present is the only era on the page that is **real and in color and still changeable**. The past is real but fixed. The future is changeable but imagined. That is why now is the moment, and the image system should make it felt.

**The darkroom gives this to us exactly.** A print goes through three trays. The *developer* brings the image up. The *fixer* stops it, and after that nothing on the paper can change. Between the two trays is the only time the printer decides how dark, how much, when to pull it. On this page:

- **Past = fixed.** Printed, archival, black and white. Nothing to decide.
- **Present = in the developer.** The image is coming up right now, in real color, and it is not finished. This is the one stretch where the reader is looking at something that can still go either way.
- **Future = latent.** Exposed but not yet visible. What comes up depends on what happens in the present.
- **The fixer is the leases.** Agreements signed in 2026 run to 2056. Whatever isn't decided in the next few years gets fixed for thirty. That is the urgency, stated in the page's own metaphor rather than as hype.

**So the present gets its own full walk through the six places, not one plate.** That makes the title literally true: six places, walked three times.

| Plate | Place | Present, 2026 (real photo, real color, unfinished) | What is arriving there now (reported) |
|---|---|---|---|
| N°01 | The river | Port of Pine Bluff today, the water and the docks | River and rail freight; the regional build-out |
| N°02 | The road | US-79 out of Pine Bluff toward Memphis | The return: families moving back South; freight demand to Memphis |
| N°03 | The field | Farmland near Sherrill with construction under way | Arkansas Cypress solar and storage; Sunflower Solar in Mississippi; data-center power demand |
| N°04 | The home | A Delta street or house today | People coming home; who can afford to stay when land values move |
| N°05 | The gathering | A real public meeting or hearing room in the county | Lawmakers and agencies paying attention; decisions being made in rooms like this |
| N°06 | The work | Pine Bluff Arsenal gate or a job site | Hanwha's $2.2B munitions campus, about 400 jobs; lithium and steel |

**What this fixes:**

1. **The $6B list stops being a dashboard.** Each reported investment is attached to one of the six places, under a real photograph of that place today. The reader sees *where* the money is landing, not a list of projects.
2. **Every present caption has an owner line, left open:** "Who will own it: *still developing.*" The past captions named who held the ground. The future captions name a local owner and turn to color. The present captions leave the line blank. The blank is the moment.
3. **The present has a visual state no other era has.** Present plates are in real color, but held at partial development (the current `saturate(.38)`) with a faint unfixed edge. They **respond to the reader's scroll in both directions** and never settle. Past plates don't move, and future plates are gated. Only the present feels live in your hands.
4. **"New owners" becomes the close of the present walk, not all of it.** The Pine Bluff storefront ("New owners") lands after the six places as the summary frame: the question in one photograph, then the fixer line about thirty-year leases, then the turn to the future.

**Photographs for the present have to be current.** The Highsmith set is from 2020, and six-year-old photos undercut "this is the moment." Shoot the six places in November–December 2026, in plain daylight color, eye level, with no staging, from public places. A good phone is fine; the honesty matters more than polish. Date every caption to the month ("Sherrill, Arkansas, November 2026"), because the newsprint frame makes the present the *news*: the dateline is the argument. Rules: no private family details, no identifiable landowners without consent, and no photographs inside the project fence. If new photos aren't ready, ship with Highsmith 2020 plates captioned honestly with their year, and swap them in as the shoot comes in.

**Optional structural move: open in the present.** Strong scroll pieces often start in the live moment and flash back (Snow Fall opens on the day itself; Firestorm on the morning of the fire). A one-screen cold open could carry a dated present photo with "Sherrill, Arkansas, November 2026. The panels are going in." Then "To see what's at stake, go back," and the landowner waiting for the bus opens the past. This gives the present the first word and the decision word. It costs the current cover's quiet. My call: **keep landowner-bus as the cover, and give it a present dateline instead of a second photo.** The masthead already says November 2026, so one line ("This November, the panels are going in near Sherrill.") under the cover photo does the job without a new image.

## 1. References: what each one does

**1. NYT, "Snow Fall: The Avalanche at Tunnel Creek" (2012)** · https://www.nytimes.com/projects/2012/snow-fall/
Six chapters follow the event's own clock. Each chapter opens with one full-bleed visual, then text takes over, with media set *inside* the reading column rather than in sidebars. ([how it was made](https://source.opennews.org/articles/how-we-made-snow-fall/))
**What to take:** one big image per chapter opening, then restraint. Pacing comes from the narrative arc, not the number of media pieces. Snow Fall's weakness, much criticized since, is media for its own sake. That is exactly the "dumped" feeling the owner describes.

**2. NYT, "What the Tulsa Race Massacre Destroyed" (2021)** · https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2021/05/24/us/tulsa-race-massacre.html
A 3D model of Greenwood is rebuilt from Sanborn maps, directories and photographs. The reader walks *one street* building by building, and each stop is a named business and owner. The destruction comes only after you have learned what was owned. ([process](https://cura.osu.edu/events/how-we-reconstructed-neighborhood-destroyed-tulsa-race-massacre-yuliya-parshina-kottas))
**What to take:** a fixed route through one place. Owners named at each stop. Ownership comes before loss. (O. W. Gurley, a Pine Bluff native, is already on the page. This is his street.)

**3. NYT Magazine, "The 1619 Project" (2019)** · https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2019/08/14/magazine/1619-america-slavery.html
One date is the spine, and every essay hangs from it. The cover is a single black-and-white photograph of empty water near Hampton, Va., by Dannielle Bowman, the landing place with nothing in it. Photographs work as ground under the essays, not as illustration of them. ([Bowman on the work](https://aperture.org/editorial/dannielle-bowman-finds-history-in-the-shadows/))
**What to take:** one organizing device that is stated once and then obeyed. A quiet, almost empty opening image can carry more than a busy one.

**4. The Guardian, "Firestorm" (2013)** · https://www.theguardian.com/world/interactive/2013/may/26/firestorm-bushfire-dunalley-holmes-family
The piece was built around Tim Holmes's photos of his family sheltering under a jetty. One family's day anchors the story, and the wider history of fire and climate is woven in around it. Chapters open on near-still video and photos, then give way to long text. ([case notes](https://bramalkema.github.io/multiplejournalism-static/case/firestorm.html))
**What to take:** one human anchor carries the large-scale story. The regional facts (the $6B, the munitions campus) should hang off people in frames, not stand as a separate dashboard.

**5. Gordon Parks, "Segregation Story," *Life* (1956)** · https://gordonparksfoundation.org/publications/gordon-parks/segregation-story
At a time when the South's poverty was usually shown in black and white, Parks shot the Thornton family in Alabama in **color**. The essay opens on a formal portrait of the elders, Mr. and Mrs. Albert Thornton. The color itself makes an argument: these are contemporaries with dignity, not a historical condition. ([LARB on the work](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/revisiting-gordon-parkss-segregation-story-60-years-later))
**What to take:** black and white versus color is a statement about *whose present* this is. That is the strongest precedent for tying color to agency instead of to scroll position. Start with people at rest and in dignity, not at labor.

**6. Dorothea Lange and Paul Taylor, *An American Exodus* (1939)**
This is the FSA photographers' own photobook. Pictures are sequenced by place (Old South → plantation → road → the West) and paired with the subjects' own spoken words as captions, not with descriptions. The route is the structure.
**What to take:** organize by place along a route, not by photographer or year. Let captions speak in the voice of the Delta where the record allows it (the current hoers-lunch caption, "6 a.m. to 7 p.m. for one dollar", already works this way).

**7. ProPublica and The New Yorker, "Kicked Off the Land" (Lizzie Presser, 2019)** · https://features.propublica.org/black-land-loss/heirs-property-rights-why-black-families-lose-land-south/
A long read on Black land loss in the South. Present-day color portraits of people *on their land* alternate with documents and archival frames. The images return to the same ground as the legal story moves forward.
**What to take:** the subject (who keeps the ground) is shot where it happens. The present is shown in color, plainly, without heroics. Tonally, this is the closest match to "rooted, plainspoken."

**8. Rephotography and then-and-now sliders (e.g. the Guardian's Civil War then-and-now; Klett's Rephotographic Survey)** · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rephotography
Same vantage, two times, with a slider or crossfade between them. It is powerful for physical change. It also falls into the same trap every time: the slider *is* the story, so the reader scrubs and leaves.
**What to take, as a warning:** this is what a past/future "pairs" layout turns into. The owner has already rejected static sliders. The rhyme should be felt across the scroll, not operated with a handle.

**9. The Bend, Chattanooga (owner's own reference)**
One site is shown at Year 1 → 5 → 10. The frame stays fixed while the place fills in.
**What to take:** the *same frame over time* is the image system that matches a 30-year lease. It is the right idea for the Arkansas Cypress view ("The land / The build / What remains") and the wrong idea to stretch across the whole Delta, where there is no single site.

(Searched and not used: a WaPo Greenwood before/after piece couldn't be confirmed. No Pudding photo-led piece fits better than the above.)

## 2. Three candidate architectures

The same inventory applies to all three. **Past (FSA):** landowner-bus, steamer-capitol, church-shoes, cotton-chop, church-interior, walker-home, walker-kitchen, walker-cowhome, walker-barn, hoers-lunch, lange-cotton, juke-afternoon, juke-dance, juke-night, haynes-1919. **Present (Highsmith 2020, desaturated):** pb-theatre ("New owners"), uapb-clock, pb-mural. **Future, round 1 (people, eye level):** 20 workshop training, 21 porch with backup power in a storm, 22 community meeting with the numbers on a screen, 23 homestead porch with rooftop solar, 24 elder and granddaughter in a field with drone and robot, 25 corner store and electric cart, 26 river port crew, 27 roadside stop with a driverless truck passing. **Future, published (`r-*`):** aerials and product renders.

### A. The darkroom tray (one continuous frame)

- **Opening:** a single sticky frame fills the screen. Blank paper fades up to the landowner waiting for the bus (Lange, 1937). The headline and "30" set over it.
- **Image placement:** one fixed frame for the whole page. Text scrolls past it, and the frame swaps or cross-dissolves at each beat: river → field → home → juke → present storefront → port → field → porch. In effect it becomes one long slow dissolve.
- **What triggers color:** position on the page. Past frames are fixed black and white, present frames are half-saturated, and future frames bloom to color as each enters.
- **Ending:** the frame lands on the homestead porch (23) in full color, and the closing line appears under it.
- **Risks:** sticky full-screen media on phones (the primary audience) is fragile and hides the copy. One frame at a time loses the FSA contact-sheet texture. Too many swaps feel like a slideshow. Hardest to build well and to make work with reduced motion. Strong metaphor, weak for reading.

### B. Chaptered photo essay (Snow Fall / Firestorm)

- **Opening:** a cover image (landowner-bus) with the masthead and thesis.
- **Image placement:** each chapter (past, present, future) opens with one full-bleed lead plate. Then text, with two or three supporting photos set in the column. Future items each get one concept image.
- **What triggers color:** the chapter. The past is black and white. The present is muted color. The future develops on entry.
- **Ending:** text and the initiative card.
- **Risks:** this is roughly the page today, and it is why the page feels dumped. Without a governing rule for *which* image goes where, chapters fill up with whatever is strong. Past and future don't talk to each other. It lowers the risk without fixing the problem.

### C. Contact sheet and archive (plates, numbered and filed)

- **Opening:** a contact sheet of the six past plates, small and numbered N°01–06, with a grease-pencil circle on one (the landowner). The reader starts inside an archive.
- **Image placement:** the page is a filing order. Each plate gets a short section: the photograph, a caption in the subject's own words where the record allows, and one line on what it shows about ownership. The present is a single "unfiled" print (pb-theatre, "New owners"). The future is a second contact sheet of the same six numbers, still blank.
- **What triggers color:** each future plate develops from blank paper → black and white → color as its section is read. The future sheet fills in over the course of the section.
- **Ending:** the future contact sheet, now full, shown next to the past one as two strips, the only side-by-side moment on the page and earned by the time you reach it.
- **Risks:** it can feel clinical, like a museum catalog. Numbered plates suit insiders and can lose a first-time visitor on a phone. Two strips at the end are a small "pairs" moment, which is fine because it is a summary rather than the structure.

### (D. Rhyming pairs, the current open question)

Past and future side by side or stacked at each beat. Covered under section 3. Not recommended as the structure.

## 3. Recommendation: "Six places, walked three times" (B's chapters + C's plate numbering, driven by A's darkroom)

### The rule that decides every image

> The Delta view shows **six places**. Each place appears once in the past (black and white, real), once in the future (color, imagined, people at eye level), and the present stops at the place the decision hinges on. An image that is not one of the six places, and is not the opening or closing frame, does not go on the Delta view.

### The six places and their plates

| Plate | Place | Past (fixed, black and white) | Future (develops) | The rhyme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N°01 | **The river** | steamer-capitol (1935) | 26, river port crew | The same water: freight leaving, then freight handled by people trained here |
| N°02 | **The road** | church-shoes (barefoot on the gravel road) | 27, roadside stop on US-79 | The road out, then a stop on the road that someone local owns |
| N°03 | **The field** | cotton-chop (Clarksdale) | 24, elder and granddaughter, drone over the rows | Labor in a line, then a family reading its own field |
| N°04 | **The home** | walker-home (Belzoni, tenant-purchase) | 23, homestead porch with rooftop solar | "Ownership has been done here before," then done again |
| N°05 | **The gathering** | church-interior (empty pews) | 22, community meeting, the numbers on screen | The room where decisions were made, now full and doing arithmetic |
| N°06 | **The work** | hoers-lunch ("$1 a day … left nothing behind") | 20, workshop training on battery systems | Work that left nothing, then work that leaves a skill |

The bookends sit outside the six. **Open on** landowner-bus: a landowner *waiting* for the bus to town. **Close on** 21: neighbors on a porch with the lights on during a storm because they own the backup power ("able to stand when they go down"). The page moves from waiting on someone else's transport to standing on your own power. Use 25 (corner cart) as a seventh plate only if the copy keeps "Neighborhood logistics". Otherwise hold it for Arkansas Cypress.

### Where the remaining images go

- **Keep, but as supporting texture inside a plate, never as a lead:** walker-kitchen and walker-cowhome (inside N°04, the strongest proof sequence on the page). juke-afternoon, juke-dance and juke-night as **one small contact strip** inside "What we made traveled". Haynes as a portrait thumbnail.
- **Present (superseded by the revision above):** the present walks all six places in current 2026 photographs. **pb-theatre ("New owners") is the closing frame of the present walk.** Show it larger than anything else in Part II, and stop it half-developed (the current `saturate(.38)` is right) with the line "The question is who the next owners will be." Move uapb-clock to the capability argument (or use it as the N°05 or N°06 stand-in until new photos exist). Move pb-mural out: a painting of the past inside a photograph of the present is a third time layer that blurs the spine.
- **Cut from the Delta view:** lange-cotton. It duplicates N°03, and the "Development" section can be carried by the type and the blank paper itself. Cut walker-barn from the Delta view (it is already used on Arkansas Cypress). Cut all `r-*` aerials and product renders (river-port aerial, us79 trucks, charging hub, greenline, mule, critter, scout, dockrunner, spindle). They are machines from above. If any belong anywhere, it is a small "what the equipment looks like" note in the initiative view, labeled as a concept.

### Order of the scroll (for the copy session)

1. **Cover:** landowner-bus, blank → print. Masthead, "30", the thesis line. No other image.
2. **I · The past.** A thin contact strip of plates N°01–06 in black and white sits under the part head, so the reader sees the six places once. Then walk them: N°01 river ("the Delta was always a source"), N°02 road (and the people who left by it: Gurley, Haynes, Williams), N°03 field, N°04 home ("some families kept the ground", with the kitchen and cow as supporting frames), N°05 gathering, N°06 work. Close the past with the juke strip ("what we made traveled") as the release.
3. **II · The present ("in the developer").** Open with the one-county number ("Six billion dollars. One county."). Then walk the six places again in dated 2026 color, each plate carrying the reported investment arriving there and an open owner line: "Who will own it: still developing." Close the present walk on "New owners," then the fixer line: the agreements signed now run to 2056, and what isn't decided in the next few years is fixed for thirty.
4. **The darkroom turn.** "Development" as type on blank paper. State the rule here, once: *the past is fixed. The present is in the developer. In the next part, a picture only comes up in full color where the people here own a piece of it.* (This is copy the rewrite session should own. It is the hinge of the whole page.)
5. **III · The future.** The same contact strip returns *blank*, as six empty frames numbered N°01–06. Then walk the same six places in the same order. Each future plate starts as blank paper and develops to black and white as it enters. It reaches **full color only at the caption line that names the owner** ("run and maintained by people trained here", "stops that local operators own", "families who keep their land"). After the walk, the strip at the top of Part III is full and in color.
6. **Close:** plate 21 (the porch, lights on in the storm). "The Delta has always been a source. The question now is what should remain, at the source." Then the Arkansas Cypress card.

### What triggers black and white → color (spec for the build)

- Today: `--p` comes from scroll position alone (`develop()` at line ~942).
- Proposed: two stages. **(a)** Blank → black and white on scroll entry, as now. **(b)** Black and white → color is gated by a marker. Put `data-owns` on the caption line that names the owner. Color starts when that line crosses roughly 60% of the viewport height and completes over about 25% of the viewport. If the reader scrolls back up, the image falls back to black and white. With reduced motion, show the final state (color) with the owner line visible.
- Past images never develop: they are printed. Present plates are live: real color held near partial saturation, gently tracking scroll position in both directions and never settling, with a faint unfixed edge. They never reach full color, because the question is open.
- Keep it to one image developing on screen at a time. Two images blooming at once is noise.

### Why this answers the owner's question

- **How do the images fit the total story?** Each one is a place in the Delta at a time, and the six places are the argument: river, road, field, home, gathering and work are where value has left the Delta, and where it could stay.
- **How do they fit the scroll?** The reader walks the same route twice. The second walk is recognizable because the first one built the map (the strip, the plate numbers, the order).
- **How do they fit the reveal?** Color is ownership. A future image is only "fully developed" where someone local owns it. The reader watches the thesis happen in the image, not just in the copy.

### Risks and what to do about each

1. **AI future images next to real FSA negatives.** This is an ethics and credibility risk. Mitigations: (a) never show them adjacent, which is why no pairs; (b) keep a visible "Imagined · not planned" label on every future plate; (c) **don't regenerate the futures in a 1940s Kodachrome look.** The current caption note says they will be. Real 1940s FSA color (Delano, Lee) exists in the same Library of Congress archive, so a Kodachrome-styled fake can be mistaken for a real record. Use a contemporary documentary color look so the eras can't be confused. *This is a decision for the owner, flagged rather than settled here.*
2. **Round-1 image fidelity.** Several round-1 images have the polished, evenly lit look of generated imagery. Regenerate to match the six-place table (same eye level, natural light, no logos), and give each one the same crop orientation as its past plate so the rhyme reads.
3. **Mobile.** No sticky full-screen stage. Plates scroll in the column at full width. The two contact strips are a horizontal row of six small frames, which fits a 375px screen at about 56px each.
4. **Image count.** With the present walk, the Delta view holds about 21 images (six past, six present, six future, cover, "New owners", close) plus small supporting frames. That is about the same as today, but every image now has a place and a time. Governed beats fewer.
5. **The present walk depends on a photo shoot.** Six current photographs of public places, dated November–December 2026. Until they exist, use Highsmith 2020 with honest year captions. Don't use AI for the present, ever. It is the one era that must be documentary.
6. **The rule is only as strong as the copy.** If a future item's text never names a local owner, its image never reaches color. That is intentional pressure on the copy, and the rewrite session should know it.

### Arkansas Cypress view (brief)

Here The Bend pattern is right: **one frame of one ground over time.** Use a single plate position for "The land / The build / What remains". The past is walker-barn (open land, held), the build is solar-aerial (labeled "not Arkansas Cypress, shown for scale"), and what remains is a future frame of the *same kind of ground* with people on it. The same color rule applies: it reaches color at the line naming what stays local. hoers-lunch stays here as the "work that left nothing behind" counterpoint.

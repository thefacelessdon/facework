## Design Context

Source: Stedmon Harper's own statements in the Nov 9–10, 2026 working session (voice notes and replies), not inferred from code.

### Users
- Delta residents and landowning families (including his own), on phones, often via a shared link.
- Liaisons and partners: Entergy landowner liaisons, contractor contacts, regional institutions (SEAEDD, UAPB), civic and corporate partners. They read it to understand the idea before a conversation.
- Job: see the whole argument quickly (investment is arriving; capability and ownership decide who benefits), then open an initiative (Arkansas Cypress) for specifics.

### Brand Personality
- Three words: rooted, forward-reaching, plainspoken.
- Emotional goals: pride in the Delta's history, urgency about the present moment, belief that ownership is possible. Never pity, never hype.
- Voice: direct, concrete, Southern without caricature. Measured on energy and technology claims (a partner flagged that overselling opens "cans of worms").

### Aesthetic Direction
- Theme: light newsprint (revised Nov 10 after the user pointed to The New York Times 175 identity). Paper, indigo-black ink, one spot color (Delta clay red). Light display serif (Source Serif 4, opsz 60, weight 300) for headlines, Libre Franklin for information. One numeral mark: "30", the years ahead.
- Imagery: photography first. Emotional, analog film scans with the negative borders kept, mainly FSA photographers in the Delta (Lange, Wolcott, Lee, Rothstein, Shahn). Grayscale, printed into the paper.
- No small drawn illustrations or diagrams-as-decoration: the user rejected them outright. Ideas without photographs are written, not drawn.
- Earlier dark indigo/gold version is kept as delta-futurism.v10-dark.html.
- References: The Bend, Chattanooga (same site shown over time, Year 1 → 5 → 10); his own earlier renderings of a reimagined river port, an autonomous freight corridor on US-79, and Delta homesteads.
- Motion: things should *transition with action* between stages (panels move, people appear), never a static slider.
- Anti-references: a generic corporate community-benefits brochure; a tech-utopia pitch; anything that implies Entergy, Google or Hanwha approved the ideas.

### Design Principles
1. Ownership is the subject. Every section answers "what do we keep?"
2. Delta first, theory second. Rifkin and other outside ideas stay short and attributed; Delta history (river, rail, rural co-ops, Pine Bluff people) carries the argument.
3. Show change over time. Prefer sequences and stages over static diagrams.
4. Honest about status. "Imagined", "proposal", "reported" labels stay visible; no claim beyond its source.
5. Family-private details never appear (acreage, payments, trust, names).

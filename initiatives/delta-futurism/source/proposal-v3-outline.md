Built from Stedmon's read-through on Nov 20, 2026. The aim is something ready to react to: enough detail that Entergy says "yes, let's keep talking", and half-baked on purpose. We fully build one workstream, Food and Hospitality, the one we want to pitch, and leave open questions on the others for us to work out with partners.

## Shared story notes (carried over from the story page)

- **Follow the investment.** The investment is the up-front number; the value it produces over 30 years is larger. Show who captures that value between those two points, and how community capability lets a county capture more of it.
- **Contracts decide who gets paid. Capabilities decide what stays.**
- **Phases follow the investment:** plan → build → run and maintain → renew. Each phase opens different work.
- **Asset vs capability:** an asset is something built. A capability is something a community can do, keep doing and pass on.
- **The same operating system:** the way Facework builds capability for creators (CACHE) applies here. Find what's open, get people ready, match them, deliver, keep the record, win the next contract.

## Workstream check (are these the right five?)

What the build buys, and where each item sits:

- People and skills → **A Workforce** (build crews; then operations and maintenance jobs; then the next project)
- Meals, lodging, crew services → **B Food and Hospitality** (the pitch)
- Materials, hauling, site services, PPE, equipment rental, professional services → **C Local Suppliers**
- Land → **D Land and Family Prosperity**
- What the community keeps when the grid fails → **E Community Resilience**

Lodging is added to B explicitly. Operations and maintenance is added to A. No sixth stream is needed yet; anything new comes out of Phase 1.

## details.html folds into the proposal (Stedmon, Nov 20)

- **Retire `details.html`.** One proposal page, laid out well, replaces the second page.
- **"Who decides" becomes a roles layout.** One column per partner (Entergy, the contractor, training partners, local businesses, landowners, Delta Futurism), with three rows: *what you already do* (it stays yours) · *what the partnership takes off your plate* · *what you never give up*. The point is that this is not more work on anyone's table. Governance basics fold into one compact strip: decisions, consent and data, reporting, conflicts, no lease terms.
- **"Measured by what remains" is tied to data.** What gets counted today (jobs announced, contracts awarded, lease payments) sits next to what the gap shows we should count (residents still in the trade a year later, local firms with repeat contracts, local share of spend, families with a plan). Baselines come from public data where they exist; the other new measures are marked as revealed by the gap.
- **Precedent carries real numbers.** For example, Meta Hyperion's $875M in contracts with 160+ Louisiana firms; Big River Steel's commitment of $200M of a roughly $1B build to Arkansas firms; the 41% local hires at a Pennsylvania solar site; Google's $25M Energy Impact Fund; Steel River's $8M in commitments.
- **"If things go differently" is cut from the public page** and kept in the README as notes for us.
- **Copy budget:** cut words 15–20% across the proposal (Mike's rule) and show more visually.

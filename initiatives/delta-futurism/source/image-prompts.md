# Delta Futurism: image prompts for the future

**Revised 2026-11-10.** The first version asked for "1940s Kodachrome" and got period costume: overalls, flat caps and 1940s trucks with solar panels bolted on. That reads as a costume drama, not the future. What carries over from the archive is **the way of seeing**, not the era.

## The rule

- **Same eye as the archive.** Eye-level documentary photography in the tradition of the Farm Security Administration photographers and Gordon Parks, who started there and kept that approach for decades in color. People at the center, mid-task, unposed, photographed with dignity. Natural light. Real places.
- **Today's world, a few years ahead.** Contemporary clothes (workwear, hi-vis vests, hoodies, sneakers, church clothes), present-day Delta buildings (brick ranch houses, metal shops, shotgun houses, manufactured homes, church halls), current and near-future equipment that looks used, not new.
- **The people are Black Delta residents** of every age, unless the scene calls for a mixed room. Say so in every prompt, because the generator will not assume it.
- **Shot on film.** Medium-format color negative film, so the future reads as photographs in the same family as the archive's negatives, not as CGI.
- **No:** period costume, vintage vehicles, sepia, product shots, logos, brand names, glossy CGI, sci-fi glow, futuristic city skylines.

## Style string (paste at the end of every prompt)

> Contemporary documentary photograph in the tradition of the Farm Security Administration photographers and Gordon Parks, shot today on medium-format color negative film (Kodak Portra 400), 6x7 frame, natural daylight, honest color with soft contrast and visible film grain, eye-level, candid, people mid-task and photographed with dignity, present-day rural Arkansas and Mississippi Delta, flat horizon, humid haze. Present-day clothing and buildings. No period costume, no vintage vehicles, no text, no logos, no brand names, no CGI sheen, no lens flare.

Aspect ratio 4:3 landscape unless noted.

## The prompts

### 01 · The river port (portrait 3:4)
Present-day Black dock crew in hi-vis vests and hard hats guide a compact electric container carrier off a river barge at a small Arkansas River port near Pine Bluff. A woman operator checks a tablet. A gantry crane behind them, stacked containers, the flat wooded riverbank across the water.

### 02 · The US-79 charging stop
A driverless freight truck with a plain white trailer pulls into a small roadside charging stop on a two-lane highway through soybean and cotton fields. A Black mechanic in a work shirt and cap stands by the charging cabinet; next to it a woman runs a food truck or a plate-lunch stand with a line of customers. Hand-painted local sign on the shed.

### 03 · Neighborhood logistics
A small corner store with a metal awning and rooftop solar on a Delta town street. A Black woman in her forties loads grocery bags into a small electric delivery cart; neighbors talk on a porch next door; kids ride bikes past. Late afternoon light.

### 04 · Farms that see
A Black farmer in his sixties, in a cap and work jacket, and his teenage granddaughter in a hoodie stand at the end of a soybean row. She holds a rugged tablet; a small field robot works the row and a drone hovers above. A brick farmhouse and a metal shop behind, a pickup at the edge of the field.

### 05 · Homesteads
New homes on family land: modest, well built, with deep porches, raised floors and metal roofs carrying solar panels. A multigenerational Black family on the porch and in the garden, a grandmother shelling peas, kids running. A church in the distance.

### 06 · The co-op meeting
A community meeting in a church fellowship hall about the community's own electric cooperative. A mostly Black crowd of all ages in folding chairs; a woman standing to speak; a projector screen or whiteboard with simple numbers; coffee urn at the side.

### 07 · The outage
Dusk during a storm outage: a small rural clinic stays lit, powered by a battery cabinet beside it, while the houses around it are dark. Neighbors gather on its porch with phones charging; a volunteer with a headlamp checks the battery.

### 08 · The training floor
Young Black apprentices, men and women, in hard hats and safety glasses learning to wire inverters and battery racks inside a converted cotton gin. Old belts and rafters overhead, an instructor in her fifties pointing at a panel.

## Round 2 (2026-11-11): style string v2 and the five images still needed

Round 1 kept: co-op meeting, farms that see, US-79 charging stop, training floor, river port. Regenerate homesteads, outage and corner store; add the landowner and the Saturday night. Every prompt now names the archive photo it answers.

**Style string v2:**

> Contemporary documentary photograph in the tradition of the Farm Security Administration photographers and Gordon Parks, shot today on 35mm color negative film (Kodak Portra 400), natural uneven light, slightly muted honest color, soft contrast, visible grain, not HDR, no teal-and-orange grading, no dramatic sky. Eye-level and candid: people absorbed in what they are doing rather than smiling at the camera, some faces turned away, ordinary clutter left in frame. Present-day rural Arkansas and Mississippi Delta, present-day clothing and buildings. No period costume, no vintage vehicles, no text, no logos, no brand names, no CGI sheen.

**A · The landowner** (portrait 3:4). Compose as an echo of Dorothea Lange's 1937 photograph of a Black woman landowner sitting on a wooden bench in a grove of young pines, waiting for the bus to town. Today: a Black woman in her sixties sits on a bench at the edge of her own land, work jacket over a church blouse, a folder of papers on her lap. Behind her, rows of soybeans and, far back on the flat horizon, the dark line of a solar field she leases. She looks off across the field, not at the camera. Overcast morning light.

**B · Homesteads** (plate N°04, the home; revised 2026-11-11 to match the six-places structure). Compose as an echo of Marion Post Wolcott's 1939 photograph of Cube Walker's new farmhouse: same framing, a dirt drive running from the lower left toward a white house on the right, a big shade tree on the left, freshly worked ground in the foreground, wide flat sky. Today: a modest new house on the family's own land, with a deep porch and a metal roof carrying solar panels, and the older family house still standing behind it. A man in his forties walks up the drive carrying a toolbox toward the inverter on the side wall; his mother sits on the porch with a pan of peas; a teenager carries grocery bags in from a pickup. Late afternoon, people small in the frame as in the original, nobody looking at the camera.

**C · The outage.** Compose as an echo of the 1930s rural cooperatives that wired the countryside. Dusk after a storm: a small brick rural clinic stays lit from a battery cabinet beside it while the houses around it are dark. On the porch, a nurse checks an older man's blood pressure, a woman ladles soup from a slow cooker, a boy does homework under the one lit window, a volunteer in a reflective vest reads the battery display. Muted evening light, wet ground, no dramatic sky.

**D · The co-op depot** (replaces the corner store). Compose as an echo of Russell Lee's 1938 photograph of day laborers boarding a truck in Pine Bluff. Today: early morning at a small cooperative depot on a Delta town street, a converted brick storefront with rooftop solar. A woman in her forties, the route manager, hands a clipboard to a young driver loading crates of groceries and medicine into a small electric delivery van; another worker checks a list on the wall. People going to work they run.

**E · Saturday night.** Compose as an echo of Marion Post Wolcott's 1939 photographs of a Saturday night juke joint outside Clarksdale. Today: a Saturday night in a small Delta venue, a cinder-block room with string lights, a live band in the corner, couples dancing close, older folks at tables, a young woman filming on her phone. Warm low light, motion blur on the dancers, grain.

## Checks before an image goes on the page
- Present-day people and clothes? No period costume?
- Black Delta residents at the center?
- Would it sit next to Wolcott's 1939 Walker photos as the same kind of looking, decades later?
- No readable brand names or logos.
- Labeled on the page as a concept image, not a real place.

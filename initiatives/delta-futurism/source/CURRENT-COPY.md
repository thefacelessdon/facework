## Delta Futurism

 From infrastructure investment to community capability

 30
 years ahead / 2026 – 2056

 The river carried the Delta's music, labor and ideas to the world. Now billions are flowing back in.

 Solar fields, power plants, a munitions campus, data centers, lithium. The leases run thirty years. The question is what stays with the people who live here when they end.

 The measure

 The measure of development is not how much investment enters a community, but how much capability, agency and ownership remains within it.

Born in Pine Bluff. Among the first Black executives at a major record label, he built the channels that carried Delta blues to Black listeners across the country. The story told in Pine Bluff is that he used the rail lines, and the Pullman porters who worked them, to move the records.

## Six billion dollars. / One county.

 That is what Arkansas's Commerce Secretary says is planned across four projects in Jefferson County over the next three to five years. The same wave is reaching across the Delta.

 Defense
### $2.2B munitions campus
Hanwha Defense USA plans to build 155mm artillery propellant at the Pine Bluff Arsenal over seven years, with about 400 jobs.

Announced Sept 2026 · company projection

 Energy · Arkansas
### Solar + battery storage
Arkansas Cypress near Sherrill in Jefferson County, tied to Entergy Arkansas's power supply for Google's data center in West Memphis.

Under construction · reported 2026

 Energy · Mississippi
### Solar and new power plants
Sunflower Solar near Ruleville, a planned solar plant in Bolivar County, and a new gas power station in Greenville, all on the Mississippi side.

Entergy Mississippi · reported 2024–2026

 Data
### Data centers
Hyperscale data centers in West Memphis and central Mississippi are driving much of the new demand for power across the region.

Reported 2025–2026

 Resources
### Lithium and steel
Lithium from underground brine in south Arkansas, with first production targeted for 2028, and steel mills in Mississippi County, Arkansas.

Reported 2026 · timelines are company targets

Black families are moving back South, and lawmakers are calling for more investment here. People, money and political attention are all heading the same way.

## Who owns / the ground?

 Every place that gets rebuilt goes through this. Investment arrives, land gets bought up, and the people who held on through the lean years get priced out of the good ones. Right now the Delta has leverage in its land, its labor and its location. That leverage lasts only until it's sold.

## Development

[PHOTO: A woman with a hoe works between long rows of young cotton, a tenant house on the horizon]

 Cotton, Coahoma County, Mississippi, June 1937. For a century, development here meant someone else's crop on Delta ground.Dorothea Lange · FSA · Library of Congress · public domain

 A solar field, a munitions plant, a lithium well and a family deciding what to do with its land are all development. What any of it leaves behind depends on what the people here can do.

 In the darkroom

 Development is also what happens to a photograph: a hidden picture comes up out of blank paper. The past on this page is already printed. The future is still developing, and what comes up depends on four things the community can build now.

 Stay connected. Own the edge. Utilities run the plants and the lines. The community should own the layer closest to home: land, local businesses, backup power and its own network, connected to the big systems and able to stand when they go down. Solar is cleaner than what it replaces, but panels wear out; a thirty-year project should come with local work to repair and recycle them.

### A freight corridor to Memphis

 US-79 from Pine Bluff to Memphis as a testing lane for driverless freight, with the logistics partners already in Memphis, and charging stops along the way that local operators own.

 03Imagined
### Neighborhood logistics
Solar-roofed corner depots and small electric delivery carts that move groceries, medicine and parts around town. The machines can come from anywhere. The depots, the routes and the dispatch belong to local operators.

 04Imagined
### Farms that see
Field robots and drones that walk the rows and check crops and fence lines, so families who keep their land can farm it, or lease it, with better information than the buyer has.

 05Imagined
### Homesteads and neighborhoods
Housing and mixed-use streets designed for the Delta's climate and culture, so the families working the new jobs can live, own and build wealth where they work.

## Assets become / capabilities

 An asset is something built. A capability is something a community can do, keep doing and pass on. Tap each one.

 the proposal,

## Community Prosperity / Partnership

 Participating landowners, Entergy Arkansas, construction partners, local businesses and training institutions, starting with the work the project already needs.

 A
### Local food & hospitality
Qualified local restaurants, caterers and food trucks compete to serve construction crews, and come out procurement-ready for future institutional customers.

 B
### Energy workforce
A construction-to-career pathway in solar, electrical, battery storage, equipment and logistics, built with the contractor and established training providers, toward registered apprenticeships where available.

 C
### Local suppliers
Help local businesses understand contractor requirements, prequalify, and close gaps in insurance, certifications, staffing, equipment and working capital.

 D
### Landowner prosperity
Voluntary financial education, estate planning and business help for families who want it. Families keep full control of their decisions. No obligation on lease income or land.

 E
### Community resilience
Assess reliable power for critical facilities, connectivity, transportation and emergency preparedness through community needs assessments and technical review.

 PHASE 1
### Discover
Confirm construction needs, open procurement, workforce demand and existing commitments with Entergy, the contractor and local institutions.

 PHASE 2
### Pilot
Where a buyer is ready: food service, supplier readiness or workforce, with clear requirements, budgets and measures.

 PHASE 3
### Prosper
Voluntary landowner programs and community investment needs, with public, philanthropic and private partners.

 PHASE 4
### Learn
Document what worked, what paid for itself, and what other Delta communities hosting new investment can adapt.

 Status: discussion draft. Not an approved Entergy, contractor or Google program. No funding, partner commitment or governance arrangement is presumed. Each activity would be developed with the organizations responsible for it.

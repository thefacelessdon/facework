# Story page v3: the whole Delta (outline)

Built from Stedmon's read-through on Nov 20, 2026. Settles the structure of `index.html` before the rebuild. Facts come from the four research passes (places and weekend culture, people, investment and migration, culture today) and are cited in `sources.html`.

## Governing rules

- **The whole Delta, balanced.** Arkansas and Mississippi alternate. Pine Bluff and Jefferson County are a thread through the story, not the frame of it.
- **Every place and every person is a seed.** Each card is written so it could later open into its own story page (profiles, interviews, archive excerpts). Lists are built to grow; they are not fixed at six or ten.
- **Titles say what happened.** Plain and specific, with no stock phrases ("The Room Where It Was Decided" is out).
- **Imagined images are the point.** They carry the "Imagined" tag. There is no apology note about photographing people later.
- **Holistic in the story, specific in the proposal.** The story's present covers all of the investment returning. The single drill-down (Arkansas Cypress) is Built to Stay, linked once and clearly.
- **Phases follow the investment, not one lease.** Plan → build → run and maintain → renew. Each phase opens different work.

## Structure

### Cover
The masthead stays as it is. Fixed: the ghost button hover now turns the text red instead of white.

### I · The Past: what the Delta built
1. **The places that made the Delta.** A researched set of 6–9 places, each a type of place with a named example, alternating Arkansas and Mississippi. Examples: the landing, the levee, the rail shops, the highway, the field, the all-Black town, the church, the juke joint, the radio station, the college. Each card has a year, a plain title and one verified line.
2. **Ideas that traveled out.** A scrolling list of about 10 people born in the Delta who carried an idea or institution out into the world, from both states and across business, media, civic life, music and faith. Built to grow into profiles.
3. **Builders who stayed.** The same rigor as the travelers: about 6–8 people or groups who stayed and built (towns, co-ops, clinics, colleges, unions, farms).
4. **Saturday night and Sunday morning.** Juke joints, the Chitlin' Circuit as Black-owned infrastructure (including Pine Bluff venues), radio, and the church as the place to meet and organize.
5. **Sum-up:** The Delta built the places, carried the ideas and made the culture. *Much of what it made was owned and banked somewhere else.*

### II · The Shift: machines, migration and the money that left
The section stays as it is, widened: both states, and the pillar cities (Pine Bluff, Greenville, Helena, Clarksdale) set side by side, with a short note on Memphis as the hub the Delta's money and people ran through.

### III · The Present
1. **The next chapter has people in it already.** The apology note is cut. Places from across the region, not only Pine Bluff.
2. **The investment is coming back.** A ledger across the Arkansas and Mississippi Delta, filterable by All / Arkansas / Mississippi, with a sourced total per state.
3. **And people are coming back too.** Return-migration data, told honestly (the region is gaining in places and still losing in others).
4. **Follow the investment.** An illustration of where a dollar of construction spending goes: a large grey share to out-of-region contractors and suppliers, and the share the region captures. The gap is community capability. Sourced where possible; labelled as illustrative where not.
5. **The kinds of work.** A horizontal scroll, as on CACHE's build page, with images of every kind of work large infrastructure needs: civil, electrical, hauling, food, lodging, site services, security, maintenance, professional services.
6. **The capabilities to build.** The five capability areas from Built to Stay, raised to the regional level: workforce, food and hospitality, local suppliers, land and family prosperity, community resilience.
7. **The phases.** Plan → build → run and maintain → renew, and the opportunities each one opens.
8. **Sum-up:** *Contracts decide who gets paid. Capabilities decide what stays.* The one bridge to Built to Stay sits here.

### IV · A Reimagined Future
1. **The places, reimagined.** The past's places come back in the same order, each one reimagined. This replaces the separate "What Life Could Become" list, which repeated it. Its best ideas (main streets, learning, culture, the connections that hold a place together) fold into these cards.
2. **Home, reimagined.** Not an antique kitchen with a battery in the closet; a Delta home designed for what comes next. The image prompt is below.
3. **Ideas travel out again.** Delta culture still moves the world (food, music, events, faith, Juneteenth). The Delta should author its own experiences and bring people here.
4. **What stays.** An asset is something built. A capability is something a community can do, keep doing and pass on. *When the investment's build is done, the skills, businesses and capabilities are still in the county.*

## Pass 2 (Stedmon, Nov 20, evening): what to change

**System rules**
- **People are always cards, in a horizontal scroll:** portrait, place and year, name, a blurb. One component for travelers, builders, families and living people. No tables or index lists for people.
- **Places and projects are cards too:** an investment card has a strong real image, the place, what it is, size and status, and a link to reporting about it. Keep the state filter. No tables on either page.
- **Present = real photographs. Future = generated images.** Generated images carry the tag **"Reimagined"** (not "Imagined"). Future images are made for each scene, not recycled across sections.
- **The scroll is the experience:** text reveals as you read; scale and centering introduce each new section; charts reveal in sequence so they culminate in one point instead of sitting side by side.

**Story page**
- Saturday night / Sunday morning: more imagery; the facts become cards.
- People coming back: first show that people **are** coming back to the South, with real numbers; then that they're not yet choosing the Delta; then the question of how this investment becomes infrastructure, services, housing and quality of life that make the Delta a place people settle.
- Where the money goes: drop "per $100". Show real dollar amounts and how they flow.
- Every kind of work: real photos for each category, not generated images.
- Five capabilities: image cards, like the proposal's workstreams.
- The culture still travels: start over. Present-day culture with imagery (*Sinners*, set in the Delta, and what it set off; living artists; festivals). Drop the Norway sister city and the stray Juneteenth line unless they connect. The sum-up has to say something true.
- The future: each reimagined place pairs visibly with its past place, using new generated images. Rewrite the narrative so "new ideas leave from here" and "what the county keeps" connect to the places and to each other.

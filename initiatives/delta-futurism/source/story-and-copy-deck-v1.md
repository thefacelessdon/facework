## 1. The story the page tells

The Delta has always been a source: its crops, its labor, its music and its people went out by river, road and rail and built wealth somewhere else. Even then, some families bought their ground and some farmers wired the countryside themselves, so ownership has been done here before. Now the money is coming back in, on thirty-year terms, and the question is who will own what is left when those years are up. "Development" is the word for that investment and also for a photograph in the darkroom. The past is fixed. The present is in the developer: real, in color, and still changing. The thirty-year agreements being signed now are the fixer, and once they set, the picture stays. What comes up depends on four things we build now: skills, local businesses, land kept in families, and power at home. The page walks six Delta places three times (as they were, as they are this year, as they could be), and each future picture reaches full color only where the people here own a piece of it. The first place to try it is Arkansas Cypress, near Sherrill.

**The spine, one line per chapter**

- **I · The past (fixed):** We were a source. Some of us kept the ground. Little of the money came back.
- **II · The present (in the developer):** The money is landing in the same six places this year. Who will own it is still developing, and the leases will fix the answer for thirty years.
- **III · The future (latent):** The same six places, if we own a piece of them. Start at Sherrill.

**Two systems, one for pictures and one for the argument.**

- **Six places (the pictures).** The river, the road, the field, the home, the gathering, the work. Each is a numbered plate, N°01 to N°06, walked three times in the same order: 1930s black and white, 2026 color held half-developed, and imagined futures. Eras never sit side by side; the plate number and the place carry the rhyme. Any photo that is not one of the six places or a bookend leaves the Delta view.
- **Four things (the argument).** Skills, businesses, land, power at home. Introduced at the hinge, tagged on every future plate, and back as the parts of the Arkansas Cypress proposal. This is what ties the two views together.

**The color rule, by era.**
- *Past:* black and white, never moves.
- *Present:* real color held at partial development, tracking scroll in both directions and never settling. Each present caption ends on an open owner line: "Who will own it: still developing."
- *Future:* blank paper to black and white on entry, then full color only at its ownership line, marked `[owns]` below (the build's `data-owns`). If a future item never names a local owner, its picture stays gray. The rule is stated in the copy once, at the turn into Part III.

**Bookends.** The page opens on Lange's 1937 landowner waiting on a bench for someone else's bus. It closes on a landowner today on a bench at the edge of her own field, with the leased solar field on the horizon, and then hands off to Arkansas Cypress, where that field is.

- **Headline:** The work / ahead

- **Picker** (label: `Pick a kind of work` · `Illustrative`):

  - **Electrical** · *During:* wiring panels, inverters and batteries. · *Leaves:* licensed electricians who can maintain the plant and wire homes in the county.
  - **Site services** · *During:* fencing, mowing, security and cleanup. · *Leaves:* a local firm the plant can hire for thirty years of upkeep.

### C5 · The proposal

- **Kicker:** the proposal,
- **Headline:** Community Prosperity / Partnership
- **Body:**
  > A proposed partnership that would invite participating landowners, Entergy Arkansas, construction partners, local businesses and training institutions to start with work the project already needs. It uses the same four things as the Delta story.
- **Five parts, grouped under the four things:**
  - **Skills · A · Energy workforce.** A path from construction to careers in solar, electrical, battery storage, equipment and logistics, built with the contractor and established training providers, toward registered apprenticeships where they exist.
  - **Businesses · B · Local food and hospitality.** Qualified local restaurants, caterers and food trucks compete to feed construction crews, and come out ready to sell to schools, hospitals and plants.
  - **Businesses · C · Local suppliers.** Help local businesses learn contractor requirements, prequalify, and close gaps in insurance, certifications, staffing, equipment and working capital.
  - **Land · D · Landowner prosperity.** Voluntary financial education, estate planning and business help for families who want it. Families keep full control of their decisions. Nothing is required of lease income or land.
  - **Power at home · E · Community resilience.** Assess backup power for critical facilities, connectivity, transportation and emergency readiness, through community needs assessments and technical review.
- **Four phases:**
  - `Phase 1 · Discover` · Confirm construction needs, open contracts, workforce demand and existing commitments with Entergy, the contractor and local institutions.
  - `Phase 2 · Pilot` · Start where a buyer is ready, in food service, supplier readiness or workforce, with clear requirements, budgets and measures.
  - `Phase 3 · Grow` · Add voluntary landowner programs and community investment, with public, philanthropic and private partners.
  - `Phase 4 · Share` · Write down what worked, what paid for itself, and what other Delta communities hosting new investment can use.
- **Status line (keep visible):**
  > Status: discussion draft. This is not an approved Entergy, contractor or Google program. No funding, partner commitment or governance arrangement is assumed. Each activity would be developed with the organizations responsible for it.

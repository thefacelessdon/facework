### Quick Reference — What These Terms Mean

**Trust:** A legal structure that holds assets on behalf of beneficiaries. The trust owns the assets; the Trustee manages them according to the rules Grandma defines. Beneficiaries receive income from the trust but do not individually own the assets — which is what protects them from probate, divorce, and forced sale.

**LLC (Limited Liability Company):** A separate legal entity that owns and operates a single business. Each LLC has its own bank accounts, its own books, and its own liabilities. If one LLC fails, the losses are contained to that LLC — they do not spread to other businesses or to the trust's core assets.

**SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle):** A type of LLC created for a single deal or property. In Darius's case, each real estate transaction sits in its own SPV. This isolates each deal so that a loss on one property does not affect any other.

**Management Agreement:** A written contract between the trust (as owner) and an operator (the person running the business). It defines the operator's compensation, authority, performance expectations, and protections. It replaces informal handshake arrangements with legally binding terms.

#### Stedmon Harper — Trustee and Trust Architect

I am not an LLC operator. The Trustee cannot serve as an operator — that is a direct fiduciary conflict. My role is different: I am the trust's architect. I design the systems that operators use, enforce accountability, and ensure every LLC runs the way it should. This is not "the guy who controls the money." This is the person who builds the machine.

**What the Trustee actually does:**

- **System design** — Build the reporting templates, capital deployment process, gate criteria, and operator accountability frameworks that every LLC follows. Standardize how the trust evaluates, funds, and monitors its ventures.
- **Financial oversight** — Review monthly LLC financials, ensure books are clean, flag variances, and report to beneficiaries quarterly. The trust's credibility depends on transparent, verifiable numbers.
- **Capital deployment execution** — When the Advisory Committee approves a capital allocation, the Trustee executes it: coordinates with lenders, manages loan documentation, ensures funds reach the right LLC with the right terms.
- **Operator support** — Operators run their businesses. The Trustee builds the infrastructure they plug into: accounting systems, compliance checklists, insurance coordination, legal templates. Operators focus on revenue. The Trustee focuses on structure.
- **Compliance and legal** — Ensure all trust filings are current, LLC annual reports are filed, insurance policies are maintained, and management agreements are followed. Coordinate with the CPA and attorney.
- **Dispute resolution** — When conflicts arise between operators, between an operator and the committee, or among beneficiaries — the Trustee is the neutral party who enforces the trust document, not personal opinion.
- **Succession planning** — Identify and prepare successor trustees, document all systems so the trust does not depend on any one person, and ensure the institution outlives the individuals who built it.

**What the Trustee does NOT do:**

- Does not run any individual business day-to-day
- Does not make unilateral capital decisions (Advisory Committee approves)
- Does not override an operator's authority within their LLC
- Does not use trust resources for personal benefit

**Trustee compensation** is defined in the trust document — a written fee structure reviewed and approved by the beneficiaries. This is not a favor. It is a professional role with personal liability and fiduciary accountability.

#### The Hybrid Trustee Model

The trust document will name a **successor trustee** in case I cannot serve. I am recommending a hybrid approach:

**Individual Trustee (me)** — I serve as Trustee because I understand the vision, know the family, and built the plan. I can make decisions quickly and hold operators accountable with context, not bureaucracy.

**Professional Trust Company as Successor** — A bank or trust company is named in the trust document as the backup trustee. If something happens to me, the trust does not stop. The institution steps in, the systems I built guide them, and the trust continues operating.

**Trust administration support** — Even while I serve as Trustee, I can hire a trust administration service to handle compliance, tax filings, quarterly report formatting, and record-keeping. This costs $1K–$3K/year instead of the $5K–$10K a full professional trustee charges. I make the decisions. They handle the paperwork.

**Why this matters:** A trust that depends entirely on one person is fragile. A trust that has documented systems, a named successor, and professional support is an institution. That is what I am building.

## PART 6 — WHAT THIS BECOMES

---

### The Trust Is Not a 30-Year Project. It Is a Permanent Family Institution.

The solar lease lasts 30 years. The trust does not. The trust is designed to hold the family's assets together **across generations** — long after the solar checks stop.

The assets — land, restaurant, operating businesses — stay in the trust. They do not get divided up when someone passes. What flows out to family members is **income from those assets**, not the assets themselves.

DECISION MEMO
RESTAURANT OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE — DECISION MEMO
Decision to make: How the restaurant should be owned going forward, given the solar lease, the trust structure, and family estate planning.
What stays the same in every option
* You continue to run the restaurant day to day
* Your experience and contribution are recognized
* No one else steps into operations
* The restaurant continues to operate at Grider Field
* Your compensation and livelihood remain tied to the business
The difference between options is risk, protection, and leverage, not control.
Option A — Restaurant owned fully by the Family Operating Trust
What this means
* The trust owns the restaurant
* You are the exclusive operator under a written agreement
* Your role, pay, and authority are contractually protected
Pros
* No divorce exposure (not marital property)
* Maximum borrowing and leverage capacity
* Restaurant can support growth beyond itself
* Your role is protected by contract, not family dynamics
Cons
* You don’t personally own the asset
* Upside is contractual, not equity-based
Best if you prioritize: protection, stability, leverage
Option B — Restaurant owned entirely by you 
What this means
* You own and operate the restaurant personally
Pros
* Full autonomy
* No shared economics
* Simple
Cons
* Highest personal risk concentration
* Divorce exposure
* Personal guarantees required for growth
* Limited integration with solar strategy
Best if you prioritize: autonomy over protection
Option C — Split ownership between you and the trust
What this means
* You own a majority stake (e.g., 60–70%)
* The trust owns a minority stake (30–40%)
* You remain managing operator
Pros
* Partial divorce protection
* Shared leverage without giving up control
* Clear succession path
* Balanced risk
Cons
* Shared economics
* More documentation
Best if you prioritize: balance and flexibility

Which risk do you prefer to carry?

SCENARIOS
RESTAURANT OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE — SCENARIOS
Rights · Control · Risk · Leverage
________________


This document compares three ways the restaurant could be structured.
Each scenario answers the same questions:
* Who controls the business?
* Who benefits economically?
* What happens if relationships change?
* How much leverage does the structure unlock?
* What is protected, and what is exposed?
Scenario 1 — Restaurant is placed fully into the Family Operating Trust
Ownership & control
* The trust owns 100% of the restaurant
* You are appointed exclusive operator/manager via a written agreement
* Management agreement defines:
   * Salary or draw
   * Profit participation
   * Decision authority
   * Removal conditions (for cause only)
You run the business exactly as he does now, but with contractual protection instead of informal control.
Economic rights
* You receive:
   * Guaranteed operator compensation
   * Performance-based profit participation
* Profits beyond operations can:
   * Pay down debt
   * Fund expansion
   * Strengthen the trust balance sheet
Ownership and operation are separated by design.
Risk if family dynamics change
* Divorce risk:
   * Restaurant is not marital property
   * Not exposed to division in a divorce
* Heirs cannot remove you arbitrarily
* Your role is protected by contract, not goodwill
This scenario reduces personal downside risk.
Leverage & upside
* Restaurant becomes a financeable asset
* Trust can:
   * Borrow against cash flow
   * Use restaurant to unlock $200–300K in capital
* That leverage can:
   * Expand the restaurant
   * Support related businesses
   * Reduce personal guarantees
This scenario provides the maximum leverage.
Trade-offs
* You do not personally own the asset
* Your upside is contractual, not equity-based
* Requires comfort with formal governance
Summary
Best for: Stability, protection, leverage, and long-term continuity
Worst for: Emotional attachment to “sole ownership”
Scenario 2 — Restaurant stays entirely outside the trust (status quo)
Ownership & control
* You own and operate the restaurant personally
* Full control, no oversight
* No formal protection beyond current arrangements
Economic rights
* You keep all profits
* No obligation to share
* No automatic reinvestment discipline
Risk if family dynamics change
* Divorce risk:
   * Restaurant may be treated as marital property
   * Potential exposure depending on state law and timing
* Estate risk:
   * Asset becomes part of his personal estate
   * Subject to probate and fragmentation
This scenario has the highest personal risk concentration
Leverage & upside
* Borrowing depends on:
   * Personal credit
   * Personal guarantees
* Limits scale
* Ties business risk directly to you
Leverage exists, but at higher personal risk.
Trade-offs
* Maximum autonomy
* Minimum protection
* Limited ability to integrate with solar or trust strategy
Summary
Best for: Autonomy and simplicity
Worst for: Risk containment, leverage, and continuity
Scenario 3 — Split ownership between You and the trust 
Ownership & control
* Example structure:
   * You own 60–70%
   * Trust owns 30–40%
* You remain managing operator
* Operating agreement defines:
   * Voting rights
   * Exit rights
   * Buyout terms
   * Decision thresholds
You are both owner and operator, with a partner.
Economic rights
* You receive:
   * Majority of profits
   * Operator compensation
* Trust receives:
   * Minority profit share
   * Participation in upside
Economics are shared but weighted toward you.
Risk if family dynamics change
* Divorce risk:
   * Only your portion exposed
   * Trust’s share insulated

   * Estate risk:

      * Partial continuity through trust
      * Clear succession path
Risk is split instead of concentrated.
Leverage & upside
      * Trust equity improves financeability
      * Borrowing possible without full personal exposure
      * Some leverage unlocked, but less than Scenario 1
This is a balanced leverage model.
Trade-offs
      * Shared economics
      * More paperwork
      * Requires negotiation up front
Summary
Best for: Balance between control and protection
Worst for: Maximum simplicity

One clarifying point (important)
Putting the restaurant into the trust does not mean:
      * You lose control
      * You work “for” someone else
      * Your contribution is discounted
It means:
      * Your role is legally protected
      * The asset becomes harder to lose
      * The business can work harder financially
What this document does not decide
      * No structure is chosen here
      * No assets are moved
      * No rights are waived
This exists so Dad can decide which risk he prefers to carry:
      * Personal risk
      * Structural risk
      * Or shared risk

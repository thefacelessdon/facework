Charles Toliver Jr. \- Family Discussion Guide

These questions are intended to help the family better understand the purpose of Lenvima, the

current cancer status, expected benefits, risks, and quality-of-life considerations.

1. Is there any evidence that Mom still has active cancer right now?

"Based on her latest scans, blood work, and pathology, do you believe there is currently cancer in

her body, or are you treating microscopic disease that cannot be seen?"

1. What did the radiation accomplish?

"Did the radiation eliminate all areas that were visible on imaging, or are there still areas of

concern?"

1. Is the cancer considered cured, in remission, or controlled?

"How would you describe her current status: cured, remission, no evidence of disease, stable

disease, or active disease?"

1. Why are you recommending Lenvima?

"What specific benefit are you hoping Lenvima will provide in Mom’s case?"

1. How much benefit are we realistically talking about?

"For an 88-year-old woman with her diagnosis, what improvement in survival or recurrence risk

does Lenvima provide?"

1. What happens if she decides not to take it?

"If Mom declines Lenvima, what do you expect to happen and over what time frame?"

1. Is Lenvima replacing chemotherapy or being used instead of

chemotherapy because of her age?

"Would younger patients normally receive IV chemotherapy, and is Lenvima being recommended

because it may be better tolerated?"

1. What side effects concern you most for Mom specifically?

"Given her age and overall health, which side effects are you most worried about?"

1. How will this affect her day-to-day life?

"Do you expect Mom to remain independent while taking this medication?"

1. At what point would you recommend stopping treatment?

What side effects or changes would tell us the treatment is causing more harm than benefit?"

Most Direct Question

"Doctor, if this were your mother, would you recommend she take Lenvima, and why?"

Important Clarification

"The paperwork says the treatment intent is palliative. Does that mean you believe the cancer is incurable, or is that standard wording for this type of treatment?

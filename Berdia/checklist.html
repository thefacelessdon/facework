Business Documents

Financial Records — This Is the Big One

The restaurant's financials are the foundation for borrowing capacity. Lenders will not lend against "we think it makes $500K." They lend against documented, verifiable numbers.

What you need (in order of importance):

What "Clean Financials" Means

A lender reviewing the restaurant will want to see:

1
Revenue is verifiable — tax returns match bank deposits match POS reports
2
Expenses are categorized — food cost, labor, rent, utilities, insurance, other
3
Owner compensation is separated — how much is Stanley drawing? Is it consistent? Is it documented as salary, draws, or informal?
4
EBITDA is calculable — Revenue minus operating expenses minus owner compensation = EBITDA. This number determines borrowing capacity.

If the restaurant has been run informally (cash transactions not fully recorded, expenses mixed with personal spending, no formal bookkeeping), the CPA will need time to reconstruct clean financials. This is common for family-owned restaurants. It is not a problem — it is a task that needs to start now.

How Life Insurance Fits Into the Trust

The attorney and CPA will evaluate three options:

Option 1: Change Beneficiary to Trust
Death benefit flows into the trust at Grandma's passing. Simple to execute — just a beneficiary change form.

Downside: Death benefit may be included in Grandma's taxable estate.
Option 2: Create an ILIT
A separate trust owns the policy. Keeps death benefit OUT of taxable estate entirely.

Key requirement: Grandma cannot be the owner. 3-year lookback rule applies. More complex ($2K-$5K setup).

Best for: Large death benefits where estate tax is a concern.
Option 3: Keep As-Is
Death benefit goes directly to named beneficiaries outside the trust. No estate tax planning benefit. No trust integration. Simplest, but may not align with overall plan.

The right choice depends on:

The size of Grandma's total estate relative to the federal estate tax exemption. The death benefit amount. Whether the policy has cash value. Whether the 3-year rule for ILIT transfers is a concern given Grandma's age.

The attorney and CPA will make this recommendation. Your job is to bring the policy.

Section 8

Questions to Bring to the First Meeting

You do not need to know the answers — these are conversation starters for the attorney and CPA. Jargon is explained in plain language below each list.

Terms you may hear:

Assignment = transferring lease rights to the trust. Grantor trust = Grandma pays tax on trust income while alive. ILIT = separate trust that owns life insurance. EBITDA = restaurant profit before interest, taxes, depreciation. Gift tax = tax on large transfers while living. Estate tax exemption = amount you can pass at death before federal estate tax applies.

For the Trust Attorney

  1. Can Berdia's solar lease interest be assigned to a trust? Does the lease allow it?
  2. What type of trust is recommended — grantor vs. non-grantor, revocable vs. irrevocable?
  3. Should the life insurance policy be held inside the trust, moved to an ILIT, or left as-is?
  4. What are the estate tax implications given the total asset picture?
  5. How should the restaurant transfer be structured to avoid triggering unnecessary taxes?
  6. What successor trustee provisions do you recommend?
  7. How do we handle the 3-year lookback rule if we transfer the life insurance policy?
  8. What is the estimated cost and timeline for trust formation, LLC setup, and operating agreements?

For the CPA

  1. What is the restaurant's actual EBITDA based on the financials provided?
  2. What trust structure (grantor/non-grantor) is most tax-efficient for this family?
  3. How will solar income be taxed — as rental income, ordinary income, or other?
  4. What are the gift tax implications of transferring assets into the trust?
  5. Does Grandma's total estate exceed the federal estate tax exemption? If the exemption drops in 2026, does that change the answer?
  6. How should LLC income be structured for tax efficiency — pass-through to trust or retained?
  7. What bookkeeping system should the restaurant adopt going forward?
  8. What quarterly/annual filings will the trust and LLCs require?

Choosing the Right Professionals

Trust / Estate Attorney

Look For
  • Experience with family trusts holding real property and operating businesses
  • Licensed in Arkansas (where the assets are)
  • Familiarity with solar lease assignments and agricultural/rural land trusts
  • Willingness to coordinate with a CPA on tax structure
Ask
  • How many family operating trusts have you drafted?
  • Have you handled solar lease assignments into trust structures before?
  • What is your flat fee or hourly rate for trust formation + LLC setup?
  • Can you draft the restaurant management agreement as part of the engagement?
Budget: $8K-$15K
Trust formation, 2-3 LLC operating agreements, and management agreement. Get a written engagement letter with scope and fees before starting.

CPA

Look For
  • Experience with trust taxation and small business financials
  • Familiarity with pass-through entity taxation
  • Ability to reconstruct restaurant financials if needed
  • Willingness to coordinate with the attorney on structure
Ask
  • Can you review the restaurant's books and produce clean P&L and balance sheet?
  • What is your fee for annual trust and LLC tax filings?
  • Can you advise on the grantor vs. non-grantor trust decision?
  • What ongoing bookkeeping do you recommend for the restaurant?
Budget: $3K-$8K initial
Financial cleanup + tax structure advisory. Ongoing: $2K-$5K/year for trust and LLC filings.

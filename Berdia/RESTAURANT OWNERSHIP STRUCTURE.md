# RESTAURANT OWNERSHIP STRUCTURE

---

## Recommendation: Trust-Owned, Operator-Managed

**The recommended structure is for the restaurant to be owned by the Family Operating Trust, with Stanley Harper continuing as exclusive operator under a written management agreement.**

This is the recommendation because it provides the strongest combination of protection, leverage, and continuity — while changing nothing about how the restaurant actually operates day to day.

The reasoning is straightforward:

1. **Divorce protection.** A trust-owned restaurant is not marital property. It cannot be divided in a divorce proceeding. Personal ownership exposes the restaurant to that risk.

2. **Leverage.** The trust can borrow against the restaurant's cash flow to fund expansion or other ventures. Personal ownership limits borrowing to personal credit and personal guarantees.

3. **Continuity.** A trust-owned restaurant has a clear succession path. Personally owned restaurants become estate assets subject to probate and fragmentation.

4. **Contractual protection.** A written management agreement protects Stanley's role, compensation, and authority more reliably than informal family arrangements. It makes his position stronger, not weaker.

**What does not change:**

- Stanley continues to run the restaurant day to day
- Stanley's experience and contribution are recognized and compensated
- No one else steps into operations
- The restaurant continues to operate at Grider Field
- Stanley's livelihood remains tied to the business

**The difference is not control. The difference is where risk sits and how much the asset can do.**

---

## What Trust Ownership Actually Looks Like

### Ownership and Control

- The trust owns 100% of the restaurant (directly or through a holding LLC)
- Stanley is appointed exclusive operator/manager through a written management agreement
- The management agreement defines:
  - Base compensation (salary or guaranteed draw)
  - Profit participation (performance-based bonus tied to EBITDA targets)
  - Decision authority (day-to-day operations, hiring, vendor contracts within budget)
  - Removal conditions (for cause only — not at will, not by family vote)

Stanley runs the business exactly as he does now, with contractual protection instead of informal control.

### Stanley's Expanded Role

Under the broader trust plan, Stanley also manages the **Ancillary Services LLC** (solar site vegetation management and maintenance). Each LLC has its own separate management agreement, its own books, and its own compensation terms. This makes Stanley the family's primary operating partner — not just the restaurant manager.

His compensation across the two businesses is structured to reward performance:

| LLC | Compensation |
|-----|-------------|
| Restaurant | Base draw + profit participation (heaviest weight — he built this) |
| Ancillary Services | Smaller base + higher profit share (incentivizes winning contracts) |

**Stanley does not sit on the Advisory Committee** that approves major trust capital decisions. This is intentional — the committee decides how much capital flows to the businesses Stanley operates. Having him vote on those decisions would be a conflict of interest. His contribution is recognized through compensation and contractual protections, not governance authority.

### Economic Rights

- **Stanley receives:** guaranteed operator compensation plus performance-based profit participation
- **Trust receives:** remaining cash flow after operations and compensation — used for debt service, expansion capital, and trust reserves
- **All economics are documented and auditable**

Ownership and operation are separated by design. This is standard practice for businesses held in trusts and family entities.

### Risk Profile

- **Divorce risk:** None. Restaurant is trust property, not marital property
- **Removal risk:** Low. Management agreement requires cause for removal — not family preference
- **Estate risk:** None. Restaurant passes through trust governance, not probate

### Leverage Unlocked

- Restaurant becomes a financeable asset on the trust's balance sheet
- Trust can borrow $200K–$300K against documented restaurant cash flow
- That leverage can fund restaurant expansion, related businesses, or trust reserves
- Borrowing is at the trust level — no personal guarantees required from Stanley

### What Stanley Gives Up

- He does not personally own the asset
- His upside is contractual (compensation + profit participation), not equity-based
- He operates within a governance structure rather than informally

### What Stanley Gains

- Legal protection of his role that is stronger than handshake agreements
- Divorce insulation
- Access to growth capital without personal debt
- A clear succession framework

## Alternative Options (For Reference)

### Option B — Restaurant Owned Personally by Stanley

- Full personal ownership and control
- No shared economics or governance
- **Highest personal risk:** divorce exposure, estate fragmentation, borrowing limited to personal credit
- **No integration** with solar strategy or trust leverage
- Best if autonomy matters more than protection

### Option C — Split Ownership (Stanley 60–70%, Trust 30–40%)

- Stanley retains majority ownership and operating control
- Trust holds minority stake with participation in upside
- **Partial divorce protection** (only Stanley's portion exposed)
- **Moderate leverage** (less than trust-owned, more than personal)
- More documentation and shared economics
- Best if full trust ownership feels like too much to give up

## Key Terms for the Management Agreement

If Option A is chosen, the management agreement between the trust and Stanley should include:

1. **Term:** Multi-year initial term (e.g., 5 years) with automatic renewal
2. **Compensation:** Base draw of $___/month plus ___% of EBITDA above $___
3. **Authority:** Full operational authority for decisions under $25,000. Trustee approval for larger expenditures
4. **Removal:** For cause only (material breach, fraud, sustained underperformance against defined metrics). Not at-will
5. **Non-compete:** If Stanley voluntarily leaves, reasonable non-compete within defined geography and timeframe
6. **Expansion rights:** Stanley has first right to manage any new restaurant locations or food-service ventures under the trust
7. **Reporting:** Monthly P&L to trustee. Quarterly review meeting
8. **Exit:** If the trust sells the restaurant, Stanley receives a defined payout (e.g., 1–2 years of base compensation or a percentage of sale proceeds)

These terms protect Stanley while giving the trust the accountability and visibility it needs.

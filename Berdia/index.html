How it fits the trust

Because the total estate sits well below the federal estate-tax exemption, the goal here is not estate-tax avoidance — it is coordinated control, liquidity, and probate avoidance. The recommended path:

1. Name the trust as beneficiary. The death benefit flows into the trust to be managed under its rules (reserves, debt payoff, distributions) rather than handed out as a lump sum. Simple beneficiary-change form — no new entity needed.

2. Keep the policy in force and preserve the Living Needs rider. It is the family's first line of defense for long-term-care costs, ahead of touching the land, solar income, or Medicaid planning.

3. Treat the cash value as standby capital, not spending money. The ~$137K can back the trust's borrowing capacity now; surrender only as a deliberate decision, since it ends the death benefit and may trigger tax.

Two things to confirm with the attorney and Prudential: (1) who is paying the $173.51/month premium and whether the premium account stays positive — a 30-year-old variable policy with a loan against it can lapse, which could create a taxable event; and (2) whether to repay or leave the $7,290 loan, which currently reduces both the cash value and the death benefit.

Quick Reference — What These Terms Mean

Trust
A legal structure that holds assets on behalf of beneficiaries. The trust owns the assets; the Trustee manages them according to the rules Grandma defines. Beneficiaries receive income from the trust but do not individually own the assets — which is what protects them from probate, divorce, and forced sale.
LLC (Limited Liability Company)
A separate legal entity that owns and operates a single business. Each LLC has its own bank accounts, its own books, and its own liabilities. If one LLC fails, the losses are contained to that LLC — they do not spread to other businesses or to the trust's core assets.
SPV (Special Purpose Vehicle)
A type of LLC created for a single deal or property. In Darius's case, each real estate transaction sits in its own SPV. This isolates each deal so that a loss on one property does not affect any other.
Management Agreement
A written contract between the trust (as owner) and an operator (the person running the business). It defines the operator's compensation, authority, performance expectations, and protections. It replaces informal handshake arrangements with legally binding terms.

Stedmon Harper — Trustee & Trust Architect

I am not an LLC operator. The Trustee cannot serve as an operator — that is a direct fiduciary conflict. My role is different: I am the trust's architect. I design the systems that operators use, enforce accountability, and ensure every LLC runs the way it should.

What the Trustee Does
  • System design — Build reporting templates, capital deployment process, gate criteria, and operator accountability frameworks that every LLC follows
  • Financial oversight — Review monthly LLC financials, ensure books are clean, flag variances, report to beneficiaries quarterly
  • Capital deployment execution — When the Advisory Committee approves a capital allocation, the Trustee executes it: coordinates with lenders, manages loan documentation, ensures funds reach the right LLC
  • Operator support — Build the infrastructure operators plug into: accounting systems, compliance checklists, insurance coordination, legal templates
  • Compliance and legal — Ensure all trust filings are current, LLC annual reports filed, insurance maintained, management agreements followed
  • Dispute resolution — The neutral party who enforces the trust document when conflicts arise, not personal opinion
  • Succession planning — Identify and prepare successor trustees, document all systems so the trust does not depend on any one person
What the Trustee Does NOT Do
  • Does not run any individual business day-to-day
  • Does not make unilateral capital decisions (Advisory Committee approves)
  • Does not override an operator's authority within their LLC
  • Does not use trust resources for personal benefit
Trustee compensation is defined in the trust document — a written fee structure reviewed and approved by the beneficiaries. This is not a favor. It is a professional role with personal liability and fiduciary accountability.

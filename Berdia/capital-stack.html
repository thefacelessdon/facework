Payback & cost: No interest and no credit-score impact. At the restaurant's current card volume (~$644K/yr), 20% of card sales clears the $82,288 in roughly 8–10 months. The $9,788 fee on a sub-year payback is an effective APR in the ~30–40% range — typical merchant-cash-advance pricing. It is fast and easy money, not cheap money.
Use rule: Reserve Square Capital for short-cycle needs with a clear, fast payback (a specific equipment buy, a bridge to a closing). Do not use it as long-term financing, and confirm the borrower of record matches the trust/LLC structure before drawing. Repayment skims 20% off every card-sales day, so it tightens daily cash flow until cleared.

5 · Recommended sequencing

  1. Use cheap and free capacity first. Tap the life-insurance cash value and arrange the solar-backed bank loan before reaching for Square Capital — they cost a fraction as much.
  2. Hold Square Capital for speed, not size. Keep the $72,500 in reserve for a time-sensitive need that pays itself back inside a year; draw only the amount you can clear quickly.
  3. Stand up the trust + LLC first. Borrow as the trust/restaurant entity so debt sits in the right place and personal/estate assets stay insulated.
  4. Get CPA-confirmed restaurant EBITDA. Revenue is now verified; margin is the missing input that unlocks the largest, cheapest facility ($200–300K).
  5. Keep the land unencumbered. It anchors the whole balance sheet — borrow against income streams (solar, restaurant) and the insurance, not the dirt.

## 1) The question this run existed to settle

Run 1 scored three American software failures and placed them in three layers —
Semantics, Frequency+Sovereignty, Stability. The stated risk was that those are
simply the instrument's only attractors: *does it keep separating loci, or does it
only distinguish famous American software failures?*

Three subjects were chosen to probe layers run 1 never touched, in three different
domains, decades and countries:

| Subject | Domain | Country | Died |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seattle grunge | music scene | US (non-software) | ~1994 |
| BHS | department-store retail | UK | 2016 |
| Nokia (smartphone business) | consumer hardware / telecom | Finland | ~2013 |

Plus, per run 1's method finding, a survivor control — this time **matched**
rather than merely adversarial: a scene of the same structural type as grunge that
met commercial contact and *persisted*.

## 2) The three autopsies

Locus named from the score pattern **before** consulting insider accounts (§4).

### A4 — Seattle grunge (~1988 – 1994)

| Term | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | **2** | at collapse, energy left the circuit and did not return — bands, press and capital flowed outward to major labels and national media; the local circuit that generated the scene stopped being where value accrued |
| Resonance | **5** | globally dominant, defined a decade of music and dress |
| Structural Integrity | **2** | the scene's load-bearing structure was an anti-commercial authenticity code, and that code could not survive its own scale |
| Entropy | **4** | = 6 − SI |

**Coherence = (2 × 5) / (1 + 4) = 2.0 → AMBER**

**Predicted locus:** primary **Field (layer 2)** — status dynamics. The scene's
status ladder priced commercial success as a *penalty*, so its own success became
a status-destroying event. Secondary **Taste (layer 3)** — quality criteria that
were legible in a room of 200 became unenforceable at the scale of a global genre
label.

**Failing term:** Flow, but the *cause* sits in Field.

### A5 — BHS (1928 – April 2016)

Bought by Philip Green for **£200m in 2000**, sold for **£1 in 2015** to a serial
bankrupt with no retail experience, into administration **April 2016**, ~**11,000
jobs**, a **£571m pension deficit** against ~20,000 pensioners.

| Term | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | **2** | the circuit ran *outward*: revenue was systematically extracted to the owner rather than recirculated into the system |
| Resonance | **2** | 88 years of brand recognition, but no cultural pull left — no reason for a new generation to enter |
| Structural Integrity | **1** | £571m unfunded obligation; sold to an owner with no capacity to operate it |
| Entropy | **5** | = 6 − SI |

**Coherence = (2 × 2) / (1 + 5) = 0.67 → RED**

**Predicted locus:** primary **Frequency (layer 4)** — rights and obligations
were never locked; a £571m obligation existed with no funding model behind it.
Secondary **Sovereignty (layer 7)** — no boundary between the owner's interests
and the system's survival, so extraction was structurally permitted.

**Failing term:** Entropy, with Resonance already low. A slow hollowing with an
acute financial trigger.

### A6 — Nokia, smartphone business (~2005 – 2013)

| Term | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | **2** | the broken circuit was *internal*: bad news could not travel upward. Middle managers withheld accurate assessments; the information loop between layers did not close |
| Resonance | **3** | brand and handset loyalty still strong globally; the smartphone proposition itself was not landing, and the developer experience was poor |
| Structural Integrity | **2** | Symbian was a documented structural liability, and the org structure itself manufactured the distortion |
| Entropy | **4** | = 6 − SI |

**Coherence = (2 × 3) / (1 + 4) = 1.2 → AMBER**

**Predicted locus:** primary **Consonance (layer 7)** — top and middle management
held incompatible pictures of reality and the reporting between them was
systematically distorted. Strategy, narrative and observed behaviour decoupled
while every individual layer looked internally fine. Secondary **Stability
(layer 5)** — Symbian.

**Failing term:** Flow, cause in Consonance.

## 3) The matched control — UK drum & bass (Hospital Records, 1996 – present)

Chosen as a structural twin of grunge: an underground UK scene that met
commercial contact repeatedly across 30 years and did not dissolve.

| Term | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | **4** | the circuit closes and recirculates: label, publishing arm, booking agency, event brand, festivals — and profits reinvested into the company |
| Resonance | **4** | global reach, sustained, with an intact community identity |
| Structural Integrity | **4** | independent through the white-label → physical → streaming transitions that dissolved most peers; owns its own distribution and live infrastructure |
| Entropy | **2** | = 6 − SI |

**Coherence = (4 × 4) / (1 + 2) = 5.33 → DEEP GREEN**

**Matched-pair result: grunge 2.0 (AMBER) vs drum & bass 5.33 (DEEP GREEN) — a
2.7× separation between two structurally similar scenes, one dissolved and one
persisting.**

And the discriminating mechanism was predicted from the numbers before the record
was checked: the surviving scene scores high on Flow and SI because **it owns its
own circuit.** The record confirms exactly that — independent label plus
publishing plus booking plus events plus reinvestment. Grunge owned none of its
circuit; its value accrued to major labels and national press.

**Finding F6 — a transferable thesis fell out of the numbers, not the theory:**
*a scene survives commercial contact if and only if it owns the circuit its energy
travels through.* That is a Facework claim in Facework's own vocabulary, derived
from an autopsy rather than asserted by the canon.

## 4) Insider-account checks

| Subject | Predicted locus | Independent / insider record | Match |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grunge | **Field** — status dynamics broke; field lost the ability to read its own signal | The *Lexicon of Grunge*: on 15 Nov 1992 the New York Times published a list of grunge slang invented on the spot by a 25-year-old Sub Pop employee, Megan Jasper. Mudhoney then repeated the fake slang to Melody Maker with straight faces. When Thomas Frank exposed it in 1993, **the Times attacked him rather than correct the record.** | **Hit, and stronger than predicted.** This is a field that could no longer distinguish signal from parody, and an institution that defended the distortion instead of repairing it. |
| BHS | **Frequency + Sovereignty** — unfunded obligation; no owner/system boundary | Joint parliamentary committee: Green's "greed and disregard for corporate governance" led to the collapse; he "systematically extracted hundreds of millions of pounds" after the £200m purchase, leaving the 88-year-old company and its pension fund "weakened to the point of collapse." Termed **"the unacceptable face of capitalism."** Green later paid ~£363m to settle pension claims. | **Hit on both.** The word the committee reached for — *extracted* — is the Flow reading, and the governance failure is the Sovereignty reading. |
| Nokia | **Consonance** — layers held incompatible pictures; reporting distorted | Vuori & Huy, *Distributed Attention and Shared Emotions in the Innovation Process: How Nokia Lost the Smartphone Battle*, Administrative Science Quarterly 61(1), 2016 — a qualitative study of 2005–2010 finding that **shared fear** drove the collapse: top managers feared external competitors and shareholders, middle managers feared internal superiors and peers, producing temporal myopia and distorted upward reporting. | **Hit.** An independent peer-reviewed study locates the failure in the alignment and reporting *between* layers, which is precisely what Consonance governs. |

**Five of five locus calls held.** Combined with run 1, **ten of ten.**

## 5) Results

### The instrument is not limited to three attractors — question answered

| Primary locus | Layer | Subjects |
|---|---|---|
| Semantics | 1 | Quibi |
| Field | 2 | **grunge (new)** |
| Frequency | 4 | Vine, BHS |
| Stability | 5 | Friendster |
| Consonance | 7 | **Nokia (new)** |

**Five of the protocol's eight phases now appear as primary loci across six
subjects, in four domains and three countries.** The run-1 worry is retired: the
locus assignment is not an artifact of the software-failure genre.

### Run 1's scalar finding was too strong — partial retraction

Run 1 concluded the scalar "carries close to zero post-mortem information value,"
on the evidence that three dead subjects all landed in RED. With six dead
subjects the scalar spreads **0.5 → 2.0, a 4× range**, and the zone boundary is
crossed:

| Subject | Coherence | Zone |
|---|---|---|
| Quibi | 0.5 | RED |
| BHS | 0.67 | RED |
| Friendster | 0.67 | RED |
| Vine | 1.0 | RED/AMBER edge |
| Nokia | 1.2 | AMBER |
| Grunge | 2.0 | AMBER |
| *drum & bass (control)* | *5.33* | *DEEP GREEN* |
| *Craigslist (control)* | *10.0* | *DEEP GREEN* |

**Corrected statement:** the scalar does not separate *dead from alive* — grunge
died and scores AMBER, "holds but fragile" — but it does rank severity and speed
among the dead, and it separates both controls from all six subjects by a wide
margin. Run 1's three subjects were all abrupt platform deaths, which is why they
clustered. **The clustering was a sample artifact, not a property of the
equation.** Recorded as a correction, not a nuance: run 1 overstated it.

### New finding — the instrument is unstable exactly where it matters

**F7.** Grunge (Flow 2, Resonance 5) scores **2.0**. Vine (Flow 1, Resonance 5)
scores **1.0**. One point of Flow, at high Resonance, moves the score 2× and
crosses a zone boundary. The equation is hypersensitive to Flow precisely in the
high-Resonance region where culturally significant systems live — which is the
only region Facework's clientele occupies. Either the Flow rubric needs finer
gradations than 1–5 in that band, or zone boundaries must widen with Resonance.

### New finding — a named failure archetype, found by replication

**F8.** BHS and Vine produce the **identical locus pair** (Frequency +
Sovereignty) despite sharing no industry, country or decade — a 2010s American
social app and an 88-year-old British department store. Both are the same shape:
**an extractive parent.** Vine's creators were refused economics because Twitter
was protecting precedent across its portfolio; BHS's reserves were extracted to
enrich its owner. In both, the subsidiary's survival was subordinate to the
parent's interests, and there was no boundary preventing it.

This is what an instrument *should* do: independent subjects converging on one
signature is replication, and the archetype is now nameable and detectable in a
living system. It is the first candidate pattern for **H2**.

## 6) Method notes and limitations

- **Matched controls beat adversarial controls.** Craigslist (run 1) exposed a
  defect in a *different* instrument. Drum & bass tested the equation directly by
  differing from grunge in structure while matching it in type, and it produced a
  transferable thesis (F6). Matched pairing is now the preferred control design.
- **The brief was not fully met.** Nokia is Finnish and non-American, but the
  authoritative record consulted is in English and Western-academic. This run
  sampled *non-American*, not genuinely *non-Anglophone-sourced*. All six subjects
  remain Global North. A subject whose primary record is not in English is still
  outstanding, and until one is scored the instrument's cultural range is
  unproven.
- **"Death" is underdefined for scenes.** Grunge's coherence dissolved while its
  output persisted and its participants mostly survived. Vine has the same
  property. The tracker has **no term distinguishing a system dying from a
  system's output outliving it**, and at Resonance 5 that ambiguity is what
  produces the unstable band in F7.

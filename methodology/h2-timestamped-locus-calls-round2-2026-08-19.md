## P4 — Nishijin silk weaving, Kyoto

| Term | Score | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | 2 | the generational transfer circuit is failing: a demanding craft held in family lines, with successors not arriving |
| Resonance | 2 | total sales in 2008 stood at ~**20% of the 1990 plateau** and have declined further since; kimono ceased to be everyday dress |
| SI | 2 | continuous documented decline in the number of corporations, looms and weavers in the district |
| Entropy | 4 | = 6 − SI |

Severity context: **(2 × 2) / 5 = 0.8 — RED.** Resonance < 4, so the scalar is
reportable here.

**Locus call: Current (primary) + Flow (secondary).**
The break is *not* fundamentally a demand problem, though that is how it presents.
It is that **strategic direction was never relocked** after the basis of the market
disappeared. When kimono stopped being daily dress, the industry's answer to *what
is this for now* was never decided — so every subsequent decision was made against
a purpose that no longer existed. The succession failure is the consequence, not the
cause: skill does not transfer into a system with no answer to why it should.

**Base-rate weight: STRONG.** **Current has never been a primary locus** in nine
scored subjects (0/7 among the dead). This call departs maximally from the prior.

**Resolves as CORRECT if,** by 2029-08-19, the sector's further contraction or
partial recovery is attributable to the presence or absence of a **relocked
purpose** — e.g. survival concentrating in firms that redefined their output
(interiors, art textiles, technical fabrics) while firms serving the residual
kimono market contract fastest. **WRONG-LAYER if** the determining factor is pure
input economics, a demand rebound independent of repositioning, or state subsidy.
**NO-BREAK if** the sector stabilises at present scale with its purpose question
still unanswered.

## P5 — RC Strasbourg, under BlueCo

| Term | Score | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | 3 | the club operates, but its player pathway and sporting decisions are legibly shaped by a parent group's needs |
| Resonance | 4 | deep local identity, an Alsatian institution with real supporter attachment |
| SI | 2 | no boundary insulating club decisions from parent portfolio logic; supporters publicly contest being repositioned as a feeder rather than an independent competitor |
| Entropy | 4 | = 6 − SI |

Severity context: **(3 × 4) / 5 = 2.4 — AMBER.** Resonance ≥ 4, so per F7 the
number is unstable and **the locus is the finding.**

**Locus call: Sovereignty (primary) + Field (secondary).**
**Archetype: extractive parent** — the same signature as Vine and BHS, in a third
sector. The prediction is that the rupture arrives as a **sovereignty event**: a
decision visibly taken for the parent's benefit against the club's, with no
mechanism available to prevent it. Field is secondary because the enforcement
mechanism here is *supporters* — status and legitimacy, not capital.

Structural context, not prediction: UEFA research counted **180+ clubs** in
multi-club structures by 2026, with the count up ~**40% in two years** to 300+
worldwide. Red Bull's 2005 rebrand of Austria Salzburg — name, colours, historical
symbols — produced a breakaway club founded by its own supporters. That is the
precedent for what a Sovereignty break looks like in this sector.

**Base-rate weight: STRONG.** Sovereignty has appeared **only ever as a secondary**
locus (0/7 as primary). Also a **replication test**: if the extractive-parent
archetype is real rather than an artifact of two American cases, it should fire
here.

**Resolves as CORRECT if,** by 2029-08-19, a material rupture occurs whose
mechanism is parent-over-club subordination: a transfer or sporting decision widely
attributed to parent priorities and followed by organised supporter revolt,
regulatory intervention on multi-club grounds, forced divestment, or a breakaway.
**WRONG-LAYER if** the material break is ordinary sporting failure, local finance,
or a stadium/administrative matter with no parent-subordination mechanism.
**NO-BREAK if** the club operates through the window without such a rupture —
which would be evidence that a boundary exists that this scoring could not see.

## P6 — Jamaican sound-system culture — the "holds" call

| Term | Score | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Flow | 4 | the circuit closes locally: selectors, dubplates, dances, and the pay-at-the-dance economy |
| Resonance | 4 | 60+ years, and the source of a global set of forms it does not depend on |
| SI | 4 | scene-held infrastructure; survived repeated commercial contact without ceding the circuit |
| Entropy | 2 | = 6 − SI |

Severity context: **(4 × 4) / 3 = 5.33 — DEEP GREEN.**

**Call: holds through 2029-08-19**, by circuit ownership (F6). This is the **third
region** in which F6 is tested — after UK drum & bass and Tecnobrega — and the
first where it is asserted *forward* rather than confirmed backward.

**Resolves as CORRECT if** the culture remains scene-governed and its practitioners'
income remains primarily circuit-held. **WRONG if** it consolidates under external
ownership, or practitioner income shifts primarily to platform-mediated
distribution the scene does not control. **If P6 fails, F6 is in serious trouble** —
two confirmations and a forward failure would suggest F6 describes surviving scenes
rather than predicting survival.
